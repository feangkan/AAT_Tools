## Narrative Arc

**Part A: System & Constraints (Slides 01–20)**

1. **Context**: Project scope (Nicholson St, 200 students, mixed-use)
2. **System Logic**: PBSA as operational/financial system, not typology
3. **Design Constraints**: Three competing logics; efficiency metrics (70–80% net)
4. **Design Drivers**: Operational logic (cleaning, security, management) shapes layout
5. **Community Problem**: Corridor inefficiency; historical solutions (Oxford) and modern responses
6. **Community Models**: Five typologies (cluster, vertical, hybrid, block–stack, mixed-type)
7. **Spatial Hierarchy**: Student life gradient from private to city
8. **Modularity**: Standardized room types as units of repetition
9. **Dimensional Standards**: Room module 3.2–3.6m × 6–7m; double-loaded corridor
10. **Construction Methods**: Modular prefabrication; off-site + on-site assembly
11. **MMC Alignment**: Repetition, standardization, speed justify modern methods
12. **Integration**: Component, panelised, volumetric spectrum; facade, structure, services coordination
13. **Structural Framework**: Frame aligns with modular grid
14. **Trade-offs**: Standardization gains speed/cost; loses flexibility/variation

**Part B: Procurement, Compliance & Case Studies (Slides 21–37)**

15. **What You Gain**: Speed and precision from industrialization; quality control via prefabrication
16. **Component Systems**: Bathroom pod + slab; integrated structural, service, acoustic solution
17. **Procurement Reality**: Architect as one stakeholder among many (developer, design team, contractor, manufacturer)
18. **Architectural Agency**: Where architects still matter despite industrialization (planning, relationships, community)
19. **System Integration**: Multi-layered coordination of communal spaces, structure, services
20. **Regulatory Framework**: NCC Class 3, fire separation, egress, accessibility, acoustics as compliance drivers
21. **Compliance as Design Driver**: Fire compartments and travel distances become generative layout constraints
22. **Failure Modes**: Over-optimized corridors, weak community, generic identity—cautionary examples
23. **Case Studies as Outcomes**: Real-world projects test three-logic balance; what gets optimized?
24. **Case Study 1: Scape Swanston Street**: Commercial vibrancy, streetscape contribution, urban integration
25. **Case Study 2: Little Hall**: Architectural expression, facade materiality, distinctive identity
26. **Successful Models**: Five planning types (cluster, courtyard, vertical, hub, podium+tower) that work
27. **Exemplary Design**: "Good" PBSA plan balances all three logics in one floor plan
28. **Design Strategy**: 200-bed project progression (single → cluster → floor plate → tower)
29. **Design Questions**: What drives community? Where do students meet, cook, study?
30. **Final Frame**: Is PBSA housing or financial instrument? Fundamental question for your project
