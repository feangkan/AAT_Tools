## 1. Which energy code applies — H6, Part J3, or Part J4

> **Housing Provisions Part H6** sets energy-efficiency Performance Requirements and Deemed-to-Satisfy Provisions for **Class 1 and Class 10** buildings (houses, sheds, carports).
>
> **Part J3** contains elemental Deemed-to-Satisfy Provisions for a **sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or a Class 4 part** of a building.
>
> **Part J4** contains Deemed-to-Satisfy Provisions for the building envelope of a **Class 3 and Class 5 to 9** building (and for Class 2 fabric other than the SOU-specific clauses that Part J3 already covers).
>
> — ABCB, *National Construction Code 2022*, Housing Provisions Part H6; Volume One, Parts J3 and J4, including J4D2 *Application of Part*.

The course brief lists **Part H6, J1P2, J1P3, J3D4–J3D10, J4D3–J4D7, J6D3 and J6D4** in one breath, because that list is written for a Class 2 apartment building. Our residential floors are **Class 3**. The correct map, without reopening AT1.4, is:

- **H6** does not apply. It is Volume Two / Housing Provisions, Class 1 and 10 only. We still cover it in this folio because the brief names it, and then we leave it.
- **J1P2 and J1P3** are Performance Requirements for a Class 2 SOU (or a Class 4 part). They do not apply to Class 3. The Class 3 Performance Requirement for energy *use* is **J1P1**.
- **J3D4–J3D10** are the elemental DTS recipe for a Class 2 SOU. We read them as the course headings, then apply the equivalent Class 3 fabric clauses in **Part J4**.
- **J4D3–J4D7** and **J6D3 / J6D4** *do* apply, because J4D2 points Part J4 at the envelope of a Class 3 building, and Part J6 is the HVAC DTS for Volume One buildings generally.

Footscray is **NCC climate zone 6** (mild temperate). Every R-Value and U-Value in this report is the climate-zone-6 figure, not a generic Australian figure.

| Course heading | Written for | Applies to this building? | What we actually use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part H6 | Class 1 / 10 | **No** | Noted, then set aside |
| J1P2 / J1P3 | Class 2 SOU | **No** as DTS | Covered as course headings; Class 3 uses **J1P1** |
| J3D4–J3D10 | Class 2 SOU elemental | **No** as DTS | Read, then apply **J4** equivalents |
| J4D3–J4D7 | Class 3 (and 5–9) envelope | **Yes** | The fabric pathway for Hybrid B |
| J6D3 / J6D4 | Volume One HVAC | **Yes** | Per-room AC + common ventilation |

## 2. J1O1 and J1F1 — what Section J is for

> **J1O1 — Objective.** The Objective of this Section is to **reduce energy consumption and energy peak demand**; **reduce greenhouse gas emissions**; and **improve occupant health and amenity**.
>
> **J1F1 — Energy efficiency.** A building must—
>
> (a) reduce the energy consumption and energy peak demand of **key energy using equipment**; and
>
> (b) reduce the **greenhouse gas emissions** that occur as a result of a building’s energy consumption and energy source; and
>
> (c) for a sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or a Class 4 part, improve occupant health and amenity by mitigating **extreme hot and cold weather events and energy blackouts**; and
>
> (d) for **other than** a Class 2 SOU or Class 4 part, protect occupant health and amenity by ensuring the **building envelope assists in the maintenance of acceptable internal conditions** while the building is occupied; and
>
> (e) be able to accommodate the future installation of **distributed energy resources**.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J1O1, J1F1.

J1F1(c) is the Class 2 apartment sentence — thermal resilience inside each dwelling during a blackout. J1F1(d) is the sentence that applies to **us**: the envelope of the Class 3 tower, and of the Class 6 retail, has to help hold internal conditions while the building is occupied. That is a fabric job (Part J4) before it is a machine job (Part J6).

On Hybrid B that envelope is not a generic cavity wall. Residential floors are **CLT panels with an outboard insulated rainscreen** (or equivalent), on a glulam frame, above a **concrete podium**. CLT already has some inherent R-Value as timber; the DTS still requires the *Total* R-Value of the whole roof, wall-glazing and floor construction, including thermal bridging at glulam, steel brackets and concrete slab edges. The concrete podium and core are the thermal bridges to watch: a balcony slab that continues the floor plate without a break will wipe out the wall R-Value along that bay.

J1F1(e) — future renewables — is picked up again at J1P4: roof space and a switchboard that can take PV, and cable pathways for EV charging even though this project is not a car-park building.

| J1F1 limb | Applies to | Design consequence here |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Peak demand / equipment | Whole building | HVAC controls under J6D3 / J6D4 |
| (b) Greenhouse gas | Whole building | Electric heat-pump heating/cooling; no gas to SOUs |
| (c) Blackout resilience of a Class 2 SOU | Class 2 only | Not the DTS test for our rooms |
| (d) Envelope holds conditions while occupied | **Class 3 + Class 6** | J4 fabric on CLT walls, roof, podium slab |
| (e) Future distributed energy | Whole building | PV-ready roof; EV-ready riser if parking appears |

## 3. J1P1 — energy use of a Class 3 building (and the Class 6 shop)

> **J1P1 — Energy use.** A building, *other than a sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or a Class 4 part of a building*, including its services, must have features that facilitate the efficient use of energy appropriate to the function and use of the building; the level of human comfort required; solar radiation being **utilised for heating** and **controlled to minimise energy for cooling**; the energy source of the services; the **sealing of the building envelope** against air leakage; and, for a conditioned space, achieving an hourly regulated energy consumption, averaged over the annual hours of operation, of not more than—
>
> (i) for a **Class 6** building, **80 kJ/m²·hr**; and
>
> (iii) for **all other building classifications** (including **Class 3**), **15 kJ/m²·hr**.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J1P1.

This is the Performance Requirement that actually binds our residential floors. Class 3 is in the “all other classifications” bucket: **15 kJ/m²·hr** of regulated energy (air-conditioning, heated water, artificial lighting, lifts, minus on-site renewables used on site). The ground-floor shop is a separate classified part, so it is allowed **80 kJ/m²·hr** — a looser number, because retail lighting and infiltration through doors are a different energy profile.

We are not running a full J1V3 / JV3 model in this folio. The Deemed-to-Satisfy route to J1P1 is **Parts J4 to J8** (fabric, sealing, HVAC, lighting, and the rest) plus **J9D3** where it applies. That is why the rest of this report spends its pages on J4 and J6: they are how a Class 3 building shows J1P1 without a simulation. If a later services engineer uses a Verification Method instead, **J1V1(3)** is the Class 3 option: a **4-star NABERS Energy for Hotels** Commitment Agreement, modelled greenhouse gas **less than 70% of the 5-star level**, and a PMV of **−1 to +1** across **95%** of occupied floor area for **98%** of operating hours. Student accommodation is closer to a hotel than to an office, which is why the NCC points Class 3 at the Hotels tool.

| Part of the building | J1P1 quantified cap | How we propose to show it |
|---|---|---|
| Class 3 residential | **15 kJ/m²·hr** regulated | DTS: J4 fabric + J5 sealing + J6 HVAC + J7 lighting |
| Class 6 retail | **80 kJ/m²·hr** regulated | Same DTS parts, shop-front glazing under J4D6 |
| Optional Verification Method | J1V1(3) NABERS Hotels | 4-star commitment; model < 70% of 5-star GHG; PMV −1 to +1 |

## 4. J1P2 — thermal performance of a Class 2 SOU (course heading)

> **J1P2 — Thermal performance of a sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or a Class 4 part of a building.**
>
> (1) The total **heating load** of the habitable rooms and conditioned spaces in a sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or a Class 4 part of a building must not exceed the heating load limit in **Specification 44**.
>
> (2) The total **cooling load** of those rooms and spaces must not exceed the cooling load limit in Specification 44.
>
> (3) The total **thermal energy load** of those rooms and spaces must not exceed the thermal energy load limit in Specification 44.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J1P2.

J1P2 is the clause the course lists, so it gets a slide, with the application stated in the first sentence of the analysis: **it does not apply to a Class 3 sole-occupancy unit**. Specification 44 heating and cooling load limits are a NatHERS-style thermal budget for apartments. Our studios are not required to produce a NatHERS certificate. What we *can* usefully take from J1P2, as an amenity overlay rather than as DTS, is the idea of a **heating-and-cooling budget per room** in climate zone 6: winter heat loss through the CLT wall and the balcony door, summer heat gain through the same glass, limited by the J4D6 solar-admittance cap.

Clause 58.03-1 (Standard D6) separately asks apartment developments in NatHERS climate zone 21 (Melbourne) not to exceed a **30 MJ/m²·annum** cooling load. That is a planning test, not J1P2, and it attaches because we are in ACZ1, not because we are Class 2. If a thermal model is run later, 30 MJ/m²·annum cooling is the planning number to beat; J4D6’s solar admittance of **0.07** on a Class 3 wall-glazing construction in climate zone 6 is the DTS way of aiming at it without that model.

| Instrument | What it caps | Applies here? |
|---|---|---|
| J1P2 + Specification 44 | Heating, cooling and thermal-energy load of a **Class 2 SOU** | **No** — Class 3 |
| J1P1 + Part J4 | Envelope of a **Class 3** building | **Yes** |
| Clause 58.03-1 Standard D6 | NatHERS cooling load **30 MJ/m²·annum** (Melbourne, zone 21) | Planning overlay via ACZ1 |

## 5. J1P3 — energy usage of a Class 2 SOU (course heading)

> **J1P3 — Energy usage of a sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or a Class 4 part of a building.**
>
> (1) The energy value of the domestic services of a sole-occupancy unit of a Class 2 building or Class 4 part of a building must not exceed the energy value with—
>
> (a) a **3-star ducted heat pump** (2019 GEMS) heating all spaces that are provided with heating; and
>
> (b) a **3-star ducted heat pump** (2019 GEMS) cooling all spaces that are provided with cooling; and
>
> (c) a **5-star instantaneous gas water heater** (2017 GEMS) providing all domestic hot water; and
>
> (d) a lighting power density of **4 W/m²** serving all internal spaces that are provided with artificial lighting.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J1P3.

Again: **Class 2 SOU only**. The reason to quote it on a Class 3 project is that it names the four domestic services the course wants us to think about — heating, cooling, hot water, lighting — and it gives benchmark equipment against which our Class 3 services can be judged even though the clause itself does not bind them.

Our proposed services, aligned with J1P1 rather than J1P3: **reverse-cycle heat-pump** air-conditioning in each studio (the brief allows AC; D29 already admitted most rooms are single-aspect); **central heat-pump hot water** rather than a 5-star gas instantaneous unit, because a mass-timber student building should not run gas to 200 ensuites if it can avoid it; lighting in studios well under 4 W/m² because the rooms are small and the balcony door already supplies daylight (AT2.2 §13). J1P3’s gas hot-water benchmark is a *ceiling on energy value*, not a requirement to use gas. An electric heat-pump HWS that beats that energy value is a better reading of J1O1’s greenhouse-gas objective.

| Service | J1P3 Class 2 benchmark | Class 3 proposal on this building |
|---|---|---|
| Heating | 3-star ducted heat pump | Per-studio reverse-cycle heat pump |
| Cooling | 3-star ducted heat pump | Same unit (single-aspect rooms, D29) |
| Domestic hot water | 5-star instantaneous **gas** (energy-value cap) | Central **electric heat-pump** HWS — better GHG than the gas benchmark |
| Lighting | 4 W/m² | Studio lighting below this; daylight from the balcony door |

## 6. J1P4 — renewable energy and electric-vehicle charging

> **J1P4 — Renewable energy and electric vehicle charging.** A building must have features that facilitate the **future installation** of on-site renewable energy generation and storage and electric vehicle charging equipment.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J1P4.

J1P4 does not require PV or chargers to be installed now. It requires the building not to make them impossible later. On a 6-storey Hybrid B building that means: a **roof** that can take a PV array (structure, waterproofing, access, and a spare isolator at the main switchboard), a **riser** with spare capacity for DC or AC cables from that roof, and, if any car bays are provided, a **cable pathway** from the switchboard to those bays. AT2.1 §7 already pushed parking toward bikes and loading rather than a resident car park, so EV charging is a small provision — visitor/accessible bays, or a future tenancy in the Class 6 shell — not a basement full of chargers.

The CLT roof / plant deck has to be coordinated with this. Char-design or encapsulation of the roof structure (AT2.1 §4) cannot be an afterthought once PV rails are fixed through the membrane. The DTS reading of J1P4 is a plant-zone plan that leaves a clear PV field, a labelled spare way in the switchboard, and a documented cable route.

| J1P4 feature | Required now? | What we draw |
|---|---|---|
| On-site PV | Facilitation, not installation | Roof zone clear of plant; structure sized for PV dead load |
| Battery storage | Facilitation | Switchboard space / fire-separated cupboard allowance |
| EV charging | Facilitation | Conduit from switchboard to any car bay that exists |
| Bike store | Brief, not J1P4 | Already a ground/basement priority (AT2.1 §7) |

## 7. J3D4 to J3D6 — ceiling fans and thermal breaks (Class 2 elemental)

> **J3D4 — Ceiling fans.** Ceiling fans must be installed in accordance with Table J3D4 in **climate zones 1, 2, 3 and 5**.
>
> **J3D5 — Roof thermal breaks.** A metal-sheet roof on metal purlins, rafters or battens, with no ceiling lining (or with a lining fixed to the metal roof members), must have a thermal break of **R0.2** or more between the sheeting and the metal support.
>
> **J3D6 — Wall thermal breaks.** A metal-framed wall forming part of the envelope must have a thermal break of **R0.2** or more at every point of contact between the external cladding and the metal frame, where the wall is unlined or the lining is fixed to the metal frame.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J3D4, J3D5, J3D6. These clauses apply to a Class 2 SOU or Class 4 part.

Three short points, because this is a Class 2 elemental part being read onto a Class 3 building.

**Ceiling fans (J3D4).** Table J3D4 does not list climate zone 6. Melbourne / Footscray is not a ceiling-fan DTS climate. We do not need fans in every studio to satisfy J3D4, and we should not draw them as if we did. A fan can still be a comfort extra in the communal kitchen; it is not a Section J requirement here.

**Roof thermal breaks (J3D5).** Our roof is more likely a **concrete or CLT roof with insulation and a membrane** than a shed-style metal skin on metal purlins. If a metal tray roof *is* used over the plant deck, R0.2 thermal breaks under the trays are the J3D5 / J4 equivalent detail. Insulated sandwich panels are exempt because the foam already breaks the bridge.

**Wall thermal breaks (J3D6).** A metal-framed rainscreen outside CLT needs R0.2 breaks at every clip. A timber-framed rainscreen is already a break. The Hybrid B risk is not the CLT panel; it is the **steel bracket or concrete slab edge** that bypasses it.

| Clause | Climate zone 6 / our fabric | Action |
|---|---|---|
| J3D4 ceiling fans | **Not required** in CZ 6 | Optional in communal rooms only |
| J3D5 roof breaks | R0.2 if metal on metal | Use on any metal plant-deck tray; N/A to CLT/concrete roof |
| J3D6 wall breaks | R0.2 at metal-frame clips | Rainscreen clips; watch balcony / slab-edge bridges |

## 8. J3D7 to J3D10 — Class 2 elemental roofs, walls and floors

> **J3D7 — Roofs and ceilings (Class 2 SOU).** Roof and ceiling insulation must achieve, in **climate zone 6**, **R3.5**, or **R3.0** if the roof contains reflective insulation. Reflective insulation must have a surface emittance of not more than **0.05**, sit next to a roof space of not less than **20 mm**, and in climate zones 3 to 8 be **downward facing**.
>
> **J3D9 — Wall-glazing (Class 2 SOU).** The Total System U-Value of wall-glazing construction must not be greater than **U2.0** in climate zone 6. Solar admittance must not exceed **0.14** on any aspect (Table J3D9).
>
> **J3D10 — Floors (Class 2 SOU).** A concrete floor above an **unenclosed** carpark or undercroft in climate zones 2 and 5 to 8 needs underfloor insulation of at least **R2.0**. Above an **enclosed** carpark in climate zone 6, at least **R1.5**.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J3D7, J3D9, J3D10.

These numbers are the Class 2 elemental recipe. They are useful as a **comparison** with the Class 3 figures in Part J4, which in several places are *stricter* for a Class 3 residential building than for a Class 2 SOU. That is the opposite of the story a student might expect, and it is worth putting on the slide.

J3D7’s CZ6 ceiling insulation is R3.5 (or R3.0 with a downward-facing reflective layer). J4D4’s Class 3 roof Total R-Value in CZ6 is **R3.2 downward** — a whole-construction number, not just the batts. J3D9 allows wall-glazing of **U2.0** and solar admittance **0.14**. J4D6 for a Class 3 building in CZ6 allows only **U1.1** and solar admittance **0.07** — roughly half the solar admittance, and a substantially tighter U-Value. Our facade cannot be designed to the Class 2 elemental tables and then labelled Class 3.

J3D10 still informs the **podium soffit**. If studios or common rooms sit over an open loading bay or an unenclosed undercroft, R2.0 under the slab (or under the CLT) is the Class 2 figure; Part J4’s floor table (next slides) is the Class 3 figure we actually have to meet.

| Element | Class 2 elemental (J3, CZ6) | Class 3 DTS (J4, CZ6) — the one we use |
|---|---|---|
| Roof / ceiling | R3.5 batts (R3.0 with reflective) | **Total R3.2** downward (J4D4) |
| Wall-glazing U-Value | U2.0 | **U1.1** (J4D6) |
| Solar admittance | 0.14 all aspects | **0.07** all aspects (Table J4D6c) |
| Floor over open undercroft | R2.0 underfloor | Table J4D7 — **R2.0** downward (CZ 4–7) |

## 9. J4D3 — thermal construction, general (the Class 3 fabric rule)

> **J4D3 — Thermal construction — general.** Where required, insulation must comply with **AS/NZS 4859.1** and be installed so that it **abuts or overlaps** adjoining insulation other than at supporting members; **forms a continuous barrier** with ceilings, walls, bulkheads and floors that inherently contribute to the thermal barrier; and does not affect the safe or effective operation of a service or fitting.
>
> Reflective insulation must have the airspace needed to achieve its R-Value, be fitted against penetrations, and be overlapped **not less than 50 mm** or taped. Bulk insulation must **maintain its position and thickness**, other than where compressed under cladding or around pipes and cables.
>
> The required **Total R-Value** and **Total System U-Value**, including allowance for **thermal bridging**, must be calculated in accordance with **AS/NZS 4859.2** for a roof or floor, or Specification 37 for wall-glazing construction.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J4D3.

J4D3 is the workmanship-and-bridging clause that makes the later R-Value tables mean something. Two Hybrid B details fail it if they are drawn carelessly.

First, **continuity**. Insulation that stops at a glulam, a CLT panel edge, or a concrete transfer beam is not a continuous barrier. The rainscreen insulation has to run past the floor edge, or a separate fire- and thermal-break is needed at every slab or CLT edge. A balcony that is a continuation of the CLT panel, with no thermal break, is a bridge the AS/NZS 4859.2 calculation will penalise until the Total R-Value of that bay falls below J4D6.

Second, **compression**. Bulk insulation stuffed behind a window head or crushed by a steel bracket does not count at its labelled R-Value. CLT to glazing is a junction that needs a designed packer, not a squeezed batt.

J4D3(5) is why we cannot quote a manufacturer’s CLT R-Value and call the wall compliant. The **Total** R-Value includes timber, insulation, air films, cladding, and the repeating bridges (brackets, fixings, slab edges). Specification 37 does the same job for wall-plus-glass as a single construction — which is the right way to think about a 6.0 m bay that is part wall, part balcony door.

| J4D3 rule | Hybrid B place it lands |
|---|---|
| Continuous barrier | Insulation past CLT / concrete floor edges; no naked slab edge at the facade |
| No uncounted bridges | Balcony breaks; steel rainscreen clips; glulam at the envelope |
| Bulk insulation keeps its thickness | Window heads, service penetrations, bracket zones |
| Total R / U including bridging | AS/NZS 4859.2 for roof and floor; Spec 37 for each 6.0 m wall-glazing bay |

## 10. J4D4 — roof and ceiling in climate zone 6

> **J4D4 — Roof and ceiling construction.** A roof or ceiling must achieve a Total R-Value greater than or equal to, in **climate zone 6**, **R3.2** for a **downward** direction of heat flow.
>
> In climate zones 1 to 7, the **solar absorptance** of the upper surface of a roof must be **not more than 0.45**.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J4D4.

Climate zone 6 is a heating-and-cooling climate; the DTS treats the dominant roof heat flow as **downward** (keeping summer heat out) at a Total R-Value of **3.2**. That is the whole roof, not the insulation label. A CLT roof panel with a membrane, tapered insulation, and a light-coloured finish will get there if the insulation layer is continuous and the plant-deck penetrations are kerbed and insulated. A dark membrane (absorptance > 0.45) fails the second sentence even if the R-Value is fine — so the specified finish is a **light** roof (SA ≤ 0.45), which also reduces peak cooling in the top-floor studios.

Downlights, roof-access hatches and fan penetrations eat ceiling insulation. J4D3 already said the remaining insulation has to still achieve the Total R-Value; the practical response is to keep lights out of the insulated roof plane where we can (surface-mounted in the top-floor studios, or a service void that does not punch the insulation).

The concrete podium roof — the transfer soffit over the Class 6 tenancy, if that slab is the envelope of a conditioned space above or below — is also a J4D4 / J4D7 element. It is not exempt just because it is structure.

| Roof test (CZ6) | DTS figure | Our roof |
|---|---|---|
| Total R-Value | **R3.2** downward | CLT or concrete roof + continuous insulation, bridging counted |
| Solar absorptance | **≤ 0.45** | Light membrane / ballast; no dark exposed membrane |
| Penetrations | Must not drop the Total R below 3.2 | Kerbed plant; minimise downlights in the insulated plane |
| Plant / PV | J1P4 coordination | PV field and plant split so insulation stays continuous |

## 11. J4D5 to J4D7 — roof lights, walls, glazing and floors

> **J4D6 — Walls and glazing.** For a **Class 3** building, the Total System U-Value of wall-glazing construction must not be greater than, in climate zone 6, **U1.1**. Wall components must achieve at least **R1.0** where the wall is less than 80% of the wall-glazing area, or the Table J4D6a value where the wall is 80% or more — for Class 3 in climate zone 6, **R2.8**. Solar admittance of externally facing wall-glazing must not exceed Table J4D6c: in climate zone 6, **0.07** on every aspect.
>
> **J4D7 — Floors.** A floor must achieve the Total R-Value in Table J4D7. For climate zones 4, 5, 6 and 7 (downward heat flow), a floor **without** in-slab heating or cooling: **R2.0**. A slab-on-ground without in-slab heating or cooling is deemed to achieve **R2.0** except in the cases listed. A floor with in-slab heating or cooling: **R3.25**, and perimeter insulation of at least **R1.0**.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J4D6, J4D7. (J4D5 roof lights: Total System SHGC and U-Value per Table J4D5 if a roof light forms part of the envelope.)

This is the slide that lands on the **6.0 m facade bay**. Each studio is one bay of wall plus one balcony door. That combination is a single “wall-glazing construction” under Specification 37. In climate zone 6, for Class 3, it must be no worse than **U1.1** as a whole, and its solar admittance no worse than **0.07**. Solar admittance is not SHGC; it is SHGC weighted by the glazed fraction. A large clear sliding door on a 6.0 m bay will blow 0.07 unless the glass is a **low-SHGC** IGU and/or the balcony soffit / a screen shades it. That is the energy reason for the D20 balcony, not only the planning reason: the 1.8 m balcony is a shade device for the glass that D28 needs for daylight.

If the opaque wall is most of the bay (≥ 80%), that wall itself needs **R2.8**. Outboard insulation on CLT can provide that; the CLT alone will not. If the glass is a large fraction of the bay, the wall’s own R-Value only needs R1.0, but the *combined* U-Value and solar admittance still have to hit U1.1 / 0.07 — which usually means better glass, not worse wall.

Floors: the typical residential CLT floor is an **internal** floor, not an envelope, except at the **lowest residential level** over the retail, the loading bay, or a basement. There the floor is J4D7 fabric: **R2.0** downward if there is no in-slab heating. That insulation is compatible with the acoustic topping already in AT2.1 §5; it is another layer in the same buildup, not a second floor.

Roof lights (J4D5) are unlikely over studios. If the communal kitchen is top-lit, Table J4D5 caps SHGC against the roof-light fraction so the J4D4 roof is not bypassed by a glass patch.

| Envelope piece | Class 3, CZ6 DTS | Studio / podium consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Wall-glazing Total System U-Value | **U1.1** | Spec 37 on each 6.0 m bay (wall + balcony door) |
| Opaque wall, if ≥ 80% of the bay | **R2.8** | Outboard insulation on CLT; CLT alone is not enough |
| Solar admittance | **0.07** all aspects | Low-SHGC IGU + balcony shading; not clear glass in a big sliding door |
| Envelope floor (over retail / loading) | **R2.0** downward | Insulation in the transfer / CLT buildup |
| Slab-on-ground (if no in-slab heating) | Deemed **R2.0** | Podium / basement slab |
| Roof light over common space | Table J4D5 SHGC / U | Only if the kitchen or lounge is top-lit |

## 12. J6D3 and J6D4 — air-conditioning and ventilation, on this building

> **J6D3 — Air-conditioning system control.** An air-conditioning system must be capable of being **deactivated when the area is unoccupied**; provide **separate temperature control** for zones with different heating or cooling needs; not control temperature by mixing actively heated and cooled air streams; limit reheating; provide an **outdoor-air economy cycle** where Table J6D3 requires it (climate zone 6: systems of **2000 L/s** or more); and, for a sole-occupancy unit of a **Class 3** building, **stop when a door to a balcony, patio or courtyard is open for more than 1 minute**.
>
> Time switches are required on systems of more than **2 kWr** and heaters of more than **1 kW**, **except** an air-conditioning system that serves **only one sole-occupancy unit** in a Class 2, 3 or 9c building.
>
> **J6D4 — Mechanical ventilation system control.** A mechanical ventilation system must be capable of being deactivated when the area is unoccupied. When it serves a conditioned space, outdoor air must not exceed the Part F6 requirement by more than **20%** (with listed exceptions). Systems with airflow more than **1000 L/s** need a time switch, except a system serving only one Class 2, 3 or 9c SOU.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. J6D3, J6D4.

This is where AT2.2’s D29 variation is closed. Most studios are single-aspect, so they are conditioned. The DTS-friendly way to condition ~200 Class 3 rooms is **one small reverse-cycle unit per studio**, not a central VAV system. That choice does three J6 things at once:

1. **Time-switch exemption.** J6D3(3) does not require a time switch on an AC system that serves only one Class 3 SOU. Students control their own unit.
2. **Balcony-door interlock.** J6D3(1)(f) *does* apply, and it is written specifically for Class 3: if the balcony door is open more than a minute, the unit stops. That is a microswitch on the D20 sliding door, shown on the studio detail.
3. **No economy cycle on the room unit.** Table J6D3’s 2000 L/s threshold is for a central system. A 2–3 kW wall cassette is nowhere near that. Economy cycle belongs on any **common-area** air handler (lobby, gym, communal kitchen) that does exceed 2000 L/s.

Common-area ventilation is the J6D4 job: corridor and lobby air that can be turned off by zone, outdoor air not more than 20% above the F6 rate, and a time switch if the fan is over 1000 L/s — with a 24-hour-occupancy exemption that a student building might actually use for the lobby. Carpark exhaust, if a basement happens, needs CO monitoring to AS 1668.2 under J6D4(3).
