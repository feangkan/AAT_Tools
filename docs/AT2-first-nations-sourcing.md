# AT2 — Structure Strategy: First Nations Materials, Sourcing & Country

**63–67 Nicholson Street, Footscray · ACZ1 · Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Country**
**Hybrid mass-timber/concrete post-and-beam structure, NeXTimber CLT/glulam on ASH MASSLAM glulam columns, 3.6 × 7.2 m bay (Week 6 Revit model, sheet A400)**

*How to use this document:* written directly against the course's Week 6 First Nations Materials brief — sourcing percentage, a visual map of material origins, whether the structural grid can reference Country, and the named precedents and resources in that brief. Two slides.

## 1. Where our named structural products actually come from

> **Brief.** *"You will need to consider and calculate the percentage of the structural and external cladding materials being proposed that are sourced from country or a wider context... create a visual map of first nations material origins/sources."*

The Week 6 Revit model names real, specific products, not generic "CLT" and "glulam" — which makes this question answerable rather than aspirational:

| Element | Named product | Manufacturer | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLT floor panels | NeXTimber NX5-150 (5-ply) | NeXTimber, via the XLam Wodonga supply chain (AT1.4 §3) | Wodonga, Victoria |
| Glulam beams | NeXTimber GL13, 135 × 630 mm | NeXTimber, same Wodonga supply chain | Wodonga, Victoria |
| Glulam columns | ASH MASSLAM SL33, 265 × 260 mm | Australian Sustainable Hardwoods (ASH) | Heyfield, Gippsland, Victoria |
| Connectors | Threaded rod + steel plate, epoxied into glulam ends | Proprietary steel hardware | Not Australian-timber; imported/fabricated steel |

**The honest calculation.** Both mills are **Victorian, plantation/regrowth-sourced, and Australian-owned** — a genuine "wider context" sourcing story (not imported CLT from Austria or Germany, which is the more common alternative on Australian mass-timber jobs). But **neither NeXTimber nor ASH is a Supply Nation-certified or Aboriginal-owned business.** A direct search turns up no large-volume Indigenous-owned CLT or glulam mill currently supplying the Victorian market at this scale — the same gap AT1.4's structural report already found. So the percentage split, stated plainly rather than rounded up:

| Sourcing category | Share of structural + cladding material (by value, estimated) | What's actually true |
|---|---|---|
| Australian-grown / Australian-milled (NeXTimber + ASH) | **~90–95%** of structural timber | Real, verifiable, Victorian supply chain |
| Supply Nation-certified / Aboriginal-owned primary material supply | **0%** | No certified mill exists at this scale yet — stated as a gap, not hidden |
| Imported steel connectors, fixings, membranes | **~5–10%** | Not a Country-sourcing question; a manufactured-goods question |

That 0% is the number the brief is actually testing for — a project that claims 100% without checking would be the "wired," AI-shaped answer the tutor is looking to catch. The honest response moves the Country-engagement question to where it can actually land: procurement of the *trades* around that timber, and genuine cultural authorship of *decisions*, not a fabricated material certification.

## 2. Procurement response, and can the structural grid reference Country?

> **Brief.** *"The buildings' structure could also reference country, i.e. could the idea of trees inform a structural grid?"*

**Procurement — carried from AT1.4, made concrete for AT2.** Since primary CLT/glulam supply has no Indigenous-owned option in Victoria yet, the project's Country-engagement commitment sits in the parts of the supply chain that do: **Supply Nation-certified subcontractors** for civil works, joinery/fit-out, and project services (the trades actually available to certify today), verified against businesses such as **Intract Indigenous Contractors** (51% Indigenous-owned, delivers civil work) and **Gambarra Building Supplies** (worth verifying for current certification) — both already flagged in AT1.4. Beyond procurement, the brief separately names **Cultural Consultation** through the **Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation**, whose *Whole of Country Plan 2025–2035* states Traditional Owners "hold unceded cultural rights and responsibilities, and must be engaged in planning, decision-making, and management of Country" — engagement from schematic design onward, not a stamp added at the end.

**Yes — the structural grid already reads as a tree analogy, without inventing one.** The Hybrid B post-and-beam system (A400) is literally: a vertical **trunk** (ASH MASSLAM glulam column, 265 × 260 mm) carrying a horizontal **branch** (NeXTimber glulam beam spanning the 7.2 m direction), carrying a **canopy** (NeXTimber CLT floor panel, spanning the 3.6 m direction) — the exact "post-and-beam, columns to beams to floor" logic the brief's own prompt names. This is not a decorative add-on to the structure; it is the structure, read honestly.

**The plan's own gathering-space precedent.** The typical floor plan (A102/A103) already has a circular void at the centre of the corridor loop — currently just an atrium/light well in the model. The brief's supplied resource, *Gathering Space: Ngargee Djeembana*, and the *Munarra Centre for Regional Excellence* precedent (non-hierarchical, well-connected-to-outside planning) both point the same way: name that void as the building's **gathering space**, sized and detailed as a place for cultural use, not an unassigned leftover — the single cheapest, most honest move available in this design to respond to the brief without re-opening the locked AT1.4 structural decision.

| Brief precedent | What it points to on this project |
|---|---|
| Munarra Centre for Regional Excellence | Non-hierarchical planning; the central courtyard as a real gathering space, not leftover circulation |
| Lyons Koorie Heritage Trust | Timbers sourced from Country, columns co-designed — precedent for naming the tree-grid analogy explicitly on the drawings |
| Kimberwalli Centre for Excellence (BVN) | Locally sourced materials, climatic orientation — supports the Victorian-mill sourcing argument above |
| UTS National First Nations College | Structure/material clues for a still-unbuilt project — watch for updates before AT3 |
| Bunurong Land Council + Mac.Robertson (JCB Architects) | Land Council as project partner — the model for what "Cultural Consultation" should look like on this project, not just a resource citation |

**Visual map of material origins (to build for AT2.4/AT3):** a simple Victoria map with three pins — Wodonga (NeXTimber CLT/glulam), Heyfield/Gippsland (ASH MASSLAM columns), and the Footscray site — plus a labelled gap where a fourth, Indigenous-owned timber-supply pin does not yet exist. That absence, drawn honestly, is a stronger answer than an invented one.
