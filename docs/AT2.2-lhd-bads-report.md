## 1. Why G7 and Clause 58 still apply on a Class 3 building

> **Clause 58 — Application.** The provisions of this clause apply to an application to construct or extend an *apartment development* if the apartment development is in the **Activity Centre Zone**.
>
> — Department of Transport and Planning (Victoria), *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.
>
> **G7O1 — Objective.** The Objective of this Part is to ensure that housing is designed to meet the needs of the community, including **older people** and those with a **mobility-related disability**.
>
> **Applications:** G7O1 only applies to a **Class 2 sole-occupancy unit**.
>
> — ABCB, *National Construction Code 2022*, Volume One, cl. G7O1.

Two different instruments are being asked of this building, and they apply for two different reasons. **Clause 58** applies because the site is **ACZ1**. That is a planning trigger, not a building-class trigger: the Maribyrnong scheme attaches the apartment-development standards to any apartment-style development in the Activity Centre Zone, and our AT1.2 planning report already treated the Nicholson Street project as that kind of development — student housing above commercial, with private open space, daylight and storage assessed against Clause 58. AT2.2 is the deep folio of D18–D21 and D26–D29 on the **same 6.0 m studio module** AT1.4 drew.

**Part G7** is written only for Class 2 sole-occupancy units. Our building is **Class 3 lodging**, as AT2.1 §1 set out against A6G4: unrelated students, a shared 120–150 m² kitchen, managed occupancy. We are **not** reclassifying to Class 2 in order to make G7 apply as a Deemed-to-Satisfy pathway. We are applying the ABCB *Livable Housing Design Standard* as the amenity overlay the project brief already asked for — “LHD where possible” — on every studio, which is the honest reading of a course task written for Class 2 when the locked design is Class 3.

| Instrument | Why it attaches here | What we do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Clause 58 / BADS | Site is **ACZ1** (AT1.2) | Assess D18–D21 and D26–D29 on the studio module |
| NCC Part G7 | Written for **Class 2 SOUs** only | Use as amenity overlay, not as a reclassification |
| AS 1428.1 | Accessible path + DDA rooms | Already covered in AT2.1 §6; not duplicated here |

## 2. G7P1 — Livable housing performance requirement

> **G7P1 — Livable housing design.** Each sole-occupancy unit in a Class 2 building must be provided with—
>
> (a) at least one **level and step-free entrance door** into the sole-occupancy unit from an accessible part of the floor on which it is located; and
>
> (b) **internal doors and corridors** which facilitate unimpeded movement between spaces; and
>
> (c) a **sanitary compartment** that facilitates independent access and use, and is located on the **entry level** of the sole-occupancy unit; and
>
> (d) a **shower** that facilitates independent access and use; and
>
> (e) the walls of that sanitary compartment, that shower and a bath (where installed, other than a freestanding bath) constructed so as to facilitate **future installation of grabrails**, in a way that minimises the removal of existing wall linings.
>
> **Applications:** G7P1(a) only applies to a sole-occupancy unit that is located on an **accessible floor**.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. G7P1.

Read against our typical floor, every item in G7P1 has a direct counterpart. Every studio opens off a level, lift-served corridor inside the reinforced-concrete core — there is no step at the unit door. Internal movement is a single room plus ensuite, so the “doors and corridors” test is the studio door, the ensuite door, and the clear width of the room itself. The sanitary compartment is on the same level as the entrance because the studio is a single storey. The shower is in that ensuite. Grabrail noggings are a construction detail in the ensuite walls, which on a CLT floor sit on a light timber or steel-stud lining rather than on the structural panel, so the noggings can be installed without cutting the CLT.

G7P1(a)’s “accessible floor” application is satisfied on every residential storey: both fire-isolated stairs and the lift sit in the same core, and AT2.1 §6 already established a continuous step-free path from Nicholson Street through the lobby and into that lift.

| G7P1 item | What it asks | How the typical studio answers it |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Step-free entrance | Level door from an accessible floor | Lift-served corridor, no hob at the unit door |
| (b) Internal doors and corridors | Unimpeded movement | Studio door + ensuite door on a 6.0 m bay |
| (c) Sanitary compartment | Independent use, on entry level | Ensuite on the same level as the door |
| (d) Shower | Independent access and use | Ensuite shower; hobless in DDA rooms, LHD hobless as overlay |
| (e) Grabrail walls | Future rails without stripping linings | Noggings / plywood in ensuite stud walls |

## 3. G7D2 — ABCB Livable Housing Design Standard (except Part 1)

> **G7D2 — Livable housing design.** Each sole-occupancy unit in a Class 2 building must comply with the **ABCB Standard for Livable Housing Design**, **except for Part 1**.
>
> **Explanatory information.** Part 1 of the ABCB Standard requires a step-free access path from a parking space or property boundary to the dwelling entry door. For Class 2 buildings, this requirement is already addressed by **Part D4** and the *Disability (Access to Premises — Buildings) Standards 2010*. Therefore it is not necessary to apply Part 1 of the Standard to a Class 2 building.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. G7D2.

G7D2 is the Deemed-to-Satisfy pointer: satisfy G7P1 by building to the ABCB Standard, skipping Part 1. That skip matters for us. Part 1 (the 1:14 path from the street, 1000 mm wide, 820 mm gates) is a house provision. Our street-to-lift path is already governed by **Part D4** and **AS 1428.1**, which AT2.1 §6 applied at the stricter **850 mm** clear door and **1540 mm** turning circle on the accessible path. Applying Part 1 on top of that would be double-counting, and G7D2 tells us not to.

What remains — and what this report assesses — is Parts **2 to 6** of the Standard: dwelling entrance, internal doors and corridors, sanitary compartment, hobless shower, and wall reinforcement. Those parts were written for a Class 2 apartment, but they describe construction features that can sit inside a Class 3 studio without changing the classification. That is the overlay.

| LHD Standard part | Applies via G7D2? | Applied on this project as |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 — dwelling access (street path) | **No** — G7D2 excludes it | Part D4 + AS 1428.1 (AT2.1 §6) |
| Part 2 — dwelling entrance | Yes, as overlay | Studio door off the corridor |
| Part 3 — internal doors and corridors | Yes, as overlay | Ensuite door + room width |
| Part 4 — sanitary compartment | Yes, as overlay | Ensuite WC |
| Part 5 — shower | Yes, as overlay | Ensuite shower |
| Part 6 — wall reinforcement | Yes, as overlay | Noggings in ensuite stud walls |

## 4. LHD Parts 2 and 3 — entrance, doors and corridors

> **Clause 2.1 — Clear opening width.** At least one entrance door to the dwelling must have a minimum clear opening width of **820 mm**.
>
> **Clause 3.1 — Internal doors.** Internal doorways must provide a minimum clear opening width of **820 mm**.
>
> **Clause 3.3 — Corridor width.** Internal corridors, hallways, passageways or the like, if connected to a door that is subject to Clause 3.1, must have a minimum clear width of **1000 mm**, measured between the finished surfaces of opposing walls.
>
> **Clause 2.2 / 3.2 — Threshold.** The threshold must be **level**, or have a sill not more than **5 mm** if rounded or bevelled, or a ramped threshold not steeper than **1:8**.
>
> — ABCB, *Livable Housing Design Standard 2022* (v1.3), Parts 2 and 3.

An 820 mm clear opening is typically an **870 mm** door leaf. That is the LHD Silver figure. On the **accessible path and in every DDA studio**, AT2.1 already committed to the stricter AS 1428.1 figure of **850 mm** clear — so those doors are wider than LHD, not narrower. On a standard studio, the overlay is 820 mm clear at the unit door and the ensuite door, with a level threshold (a timber or aluminium sill of 5 mm or less, or a flush detail into the CLT acoustic topping).

There is no internal hallway inside a 15–25 m² studio in the usual house sense. The 1000 mm corridor test therefore lands on two places: the **common corridor** (already 1.8–2.0 m from AT1.4 / AT2.1, well above 1000 mm) and the **clear width past the bed and wardrobe** inside the room, which must stay at least 1000 mm on the path to the ensuite. On a 6.0 m structural bay that is achievable without enlarging the AT1 studio.

| Door / path | LHD minimum | What we provide |
|---|---|---|
| Studio entrance, standard room | 820 mm clear | 820 mm clear (870 mm leaf) |
| Studio entrance, DDA room / accessible path | 820 mm (LHD) / **850 mm** (AS 1428.1) | **850 mm** clear — the stricter figure |
| Ensuite door | 820 mm clear | 820 mm standard; 850 mm in DDA rooms |
| Threshold | Level, or ≤ 5 mm, or 1:8 ramp | Flush / ≤ 5 mm into the acoustic topping |
| Common corridor | 1000 mm (LHD) | 1.8–2.0 m (AT2.1 escape width) |
| Path to ensuite inside the studio | 1000 mm | Clear strip past bed / wardrobe |

## 5. LHD Part 4 — sanitary compartment

> **Clause 4.1 — Location.** There must be at least one sanitary compartment located on the **ground or entry level** of a dwelling.
>
> **Clause 4.2 — Circulation space.** A sanitary compartment subject to Clause 4.1 must be constructed so that—
>
> (a) for a toilet pan in a separate sanitary compartment, there is a clear width of not less than **900 mm** between the finished surfaces of opposing walls either side of the pan; and
>
> (b) any fixed obstruction, such as a basin or vanity, is located at least **450 mm** from the centreline of the toilet pan; and
>
> (c) a clear minimum circulation space of **1200 mm by 900 mm** is provided from the front edge of the toilet pan, **clear of the door swing**.
>
> — ABCB, *Livable Housing Design Standard 2022* (v1.3), Part 4.

Every studio is a single-level sole-occupancy unit, so the “entry level” location test is met by putting the WC in the ensuite. The circulation figures are smaller than AS 1428.1’s 1540 mm turning circle — LHD is Silver, not specialist accessible design. Standard ensuites are set out to the LHD 1200 × 900 mm clear zone in front of the pan, 450 mm from the pan centreline to the basin, and a cavity slider or outward-opening door so the door leaf does not eat the circulation. DDA ensuites go further: they take the 1540 mm turning circle from AT2.1 §6, which is why those rooms are 30–35 m² rather than 15–25 m².

This is also the honest limit of a 15–25 m² studio. LHD Part 4 can be met in a compact ensuite. AS 1428.1 cannot, which is why the brief’s 1-in-20 DDA rooms exist as a separate, larger module on the same 6.0 m grid.

| Sanitary test | LHD Silver (every studio) | AS 1428.1 (DDA studios only) |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Ensuite on the entry level | Same |
| Width beside pan | 900 mm between walls | Wider clearances per AS 1428.1 |
| Basin from pan centreline | ≥ 450 mm | Per AS 1428.1 fitout |
| Clear zone in front of pan | 1200 × 900 mm, clear of the door | 1540 mm turning circle |
| Door | Must not swing into that zone | 850 mm clear |

## 6. LHD Parts 5 and 6 — hobless shower and wall reinforcement

> **Clause 5.2 — Hobless and step-free entry.** At least one shower must have a **hobless and step-free** entry. A lip not more than **5 mm** in height may be provided for water retention.
>
> **Clause 6.1 — Location.** Reinforcing must be provided to the sanitary compartment subject to Part 4, and to a bathroom containing a shower subject to Part 5. The requirement need not be complied with if the walls are constructed of **concrete, masonry or another material capable of supporting grabrails** without additional reinforcement.
>
> **Clause 6.2 — Construction.** Reinforcing must be **12 mm structural plywood** (or similar), or **timber noggings not less than 25 mm** thick, in the locations shown in Figures 6.2a–6.2g — typically a band of noggings between about **600 mm and 1600 mm** above finished floor.
>
> — ABCB, *Livable Housing Design Standard 2022* (v1.3), Parts 5 and 6.

“Hobless” and “step-free” are not the same thing, and the Standard requires both: a hobless shower with a step-down is still a step; a step-free shower with a hob still has a hob. The DDA ensuites are fully hobless, with the waterproof membrane of AT2.1 §5 (F2D2 / Specification 26) running across the whole ensuite floor and a 5 mm waterstop at most. On a standard studio the overlay is the same hobless detail rather than a tiled hob, because it is cheaper to repeat one wet-area detail across ~200 rooms than to run two, and because the membrane already sits in the acoustic topping on the CLT panel.

Noggings are the part of G7 that is easiest to miss in a mass-timber building. The CLT floor panel is not the wall. Ensuite walls are a stud lining (light timber or steel) on the CLT, and that lining is **not** masonry, so Clause 6.1’s exemption does not apply. Horizontal noggings 25 mm thick, or a sheet of 12 mm structural plywood, go into the stud wall behind the WC, the shower, and any bath, in the 600–1600 mm band the Standard’s figures describe. Grabrails are **not** installed now — only the blocking that lets them be fixed later without stripping tiles. Cavity sliders in ensuite walls need a framed pocket that can still take those noggings; if the pocket fights the nogging zone, the door swings out instead.

| Element | Standard | Our ensuite |
|---|---|---|
| Shower entry | Hobless **and** step-free; lip ≤ 5 mm | Hobless tray in the Spec 26 membrane |
| Waterproofing | AS 3740 / F2D2 (Class 3) | Membrane in the CLT acoustic buildup (AT2.1 §5) |
| Wall type | Stud lining, not masonry | Noggings required — exemption does not apply |
| Reinforcement | 12 mm plywood or 25 mm noggings, ~600–1600 mm AFFL | In WC, shower (and bath if provided) walls |
| Grabrails at construction | Not required | Not fitted; blocking only |

## 7. Clause 58.05-1 — Standard D18 Accessibility

> **58.05-1 Accessibility objective.** To ensure the design of dwellings meets the needs of people with **limited mobility**.
>
> **Standard D18.** At least **50 per cent of dwellings** should have:
>
> - a clear opening width of at least **850 mm** at the entrance to the dwelling and the main bedroom;
> - a clear path with a minimum width of **1.2 metres** connecting the dwelling entrance to the main bedroom, an adaptable bathroom and the living area;
> - a main bedroom with access to an adaptable bathroom;
> - at least one **adaptable bathroom** that meets all of the requirements of either Design A or Design B specified in Table D7.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-1, Standard D18.

D18 is the planning-scheme accessibility standard, and it is more demanding than LHD Silver: 850 mm doors (matching AS 1428.1, not the LHD 820 mm), a 1.2 m path, and an adaptable bathroom to Table D7 in **half of all dwellings**. Our brief’s DDA ratio is **1 in 20** (5%) at 30–35 m². That 5% fully meets Design A/B and AS 1428.1. It does **not** meet D18’s 50% figure.

This is a real conflict, not a drafting error, and it should be stated as one. A 15–25 m² student studio cannot contain a Table D7 adaptable bathroom, a 1.2 m path and a 3.0 × 3.4 m bedroom. Enlarging every second room to do that would abandon the AT1 studio module. The design response is therefore layered, not faked:

1. **Every studio** gets the LHD overlay (820 mm doors, 1000 mm path, hobless shower, noggings) — more rooms than D18 asks, at a lower specification.
2. **One in twenty studios**, on the same 6.0 m grid, is a 30–35 m² DDA room to AS 1428.1 / Table D7 — fewer rooms than D18 asks, at a higher specification.
3. The **common path** (street, lobby, lift, corridor) is fully accessible, which is the part of D18 that a lodging building can meet without turning studios into apartments.

A planning report would argue that the objective of 58.05-1 — dwellings that meet the needs of people with limited mobility — is met by a fully accessible common building plus a managed stock of DDA rooms, which is how Class 3 student housing is actually occupied. The 50% adaptable-apartment ratio remains a Class 2 apartment test that this floor plate does not claim to pass.

| D18 test | Standard | This project |
|---|---|---|
| Share of dwellings | 50% adaptable | 5% DDA (brief 1:20) + 100% LHD overlay |
| Entrance / bedroom door | 850 mm | 850 mm on DDA + accessible path; 820 mm LHD on typical studios |
| Internal path | 1.2 m | 1.2 m in DDA rooms; 1.0 m LHD in typical studios |
| Adaptable bathroom | Table D7 Design A or B | DDA ensuites only |
| Common path | (implied by the objective) | Street → lobby → lift → corridor, AT2.1 §6 |

## 8. Clause 58.05-2 — Standard D19 Building entry and circulation

> **58.05-2 Building entry and circulation objectives.** To provide each dwelling and building with its own **sense of identity**. To ensure the internal layout of buildings provide for the **safe, functional and efficient movement** of residents. To ensure internal communal areas provide adequate access to **daylight and natural ventilation**.
>
> **Standard D19.** Entries to dwellings and buildings should be **visible and easily identifiable**, and provide **shelter**, a sense of personal address and a **transitional space** around the entry. The layout should **clearly distinguish** entrances to residential and non-residential areas; provide **windows to building entrances and lift areas**; provide visible, safe and attractive stairs from the entry level; and provide common areas and corridors that include at least one source of **natural light and natural ventilation**, avoid obstruction from building services, and maintain **clear sight lines**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-2, Standard D19.

This is the same circulation AT2.1 already measured for escape, now read as amenity. The building has two identities at the street: the **Class 6 retail** frontage on Nicholson Street, and a separate **residential lobby** that is the student address — mail, parcels, after-hours access, the start of the step-free path. Those two doors must not be interchangeable; D19’s “distinguish residential and non-residential” line is the planning reason for a dedicated lobby rather than a shared arcade.

Inside, the two fire-isolated stairs in the RC core are not only an NCC exit pair (D2D3 / D2D6). D19 wants them **visible and attractive from the entry level** so residents use them. A glazed screen to the stair, or a daylit lift lobby at the core, turns the fire stair into the everyday stair. The double-loaded corridor is 1.8–2.0 m, clear of services (risers sit in the core, not in the corridor), with sight lines from the lift doors to both stair doors — the same ~24–30 m stair separation AT2.1 already set inside the 9–45 m D2D6 band. Daylight and ventilation to that corridor is the harder D19 test on a double-loaded plate: end-of-corridor windows, a glazed lift lobby, or mechanical ventilation where a mid-block corridor cannot see the sky. That last option is honest; a 20-room floor on a 6.0 m grid will not have a window in the middle of the corridor.

| D19 item | Intent | Our response |
|---|---|---|
| Identifiable building entry | Shelter, address, transition | Dedicated residential lobby, separate from retail |
| Distinguish residential / non-residential | Two entries | Class 6 shopfront vs Class 3 lobby |
| Windows to entries and lift areas | Daylight at the core | Glazed lift lobby at the RC core |
| Visible stairs | Everyday use, not only fire | Both core stairs readable from the lobby |
| Corridor: light, air, sight lines, no services | Amenity of the common path | 1.8–2.0 m clear; risers in the core; end windows or mechanical air |

## 9. Clause 58.05-3 — Standard D20 Private open space

> **58.05-3 Private open space objective.** To provide adequate private open space for the reasonable recreation and service needs of residents.
>
> **Standard D20.** A dwelling should have private open space consisting of at least one of: a ground-level area of **25 m²** (minimum dimension **3 m**); a **balcony** with the area and dimensions in Table D8; a podium area of **15 m²** (dimension **3 m**); or a roof area of **10 m²** (dimension **2 m**) — each with convenient access from a living room. If a cooling or heating unit is located on a balcony, increase the Table D8 area by at least **1.5 m²**. If the finished floor level is **40 m or more** above ground, Table D8 does not apply if the additional internal area in Table D9 is provided instead.
>
> **Table D8 — studio / 1 bedroom, orientation other than north or south:** **8 m²**, minimum dimension **1.8 m**. North (N20°W–N30°E): 8 m² / 1.7 m. South (S30°W–S30°E): 8 m² / 1.2 m.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-3, Standard D20.

Precinct 1B’s preferred height is **6 storeys / 19.2 m**. That is well under the 40 m threshold, so the internal-POS substitution in Table D9 is **not** available. Every studio needs a balcony (or equivalent), not an extra 8 m² of room. On a 6.0 m facade bay, an 8 m² balcony at 1.8 m minimum dimension is a balcony about **6.0 m wide × 1.8 m deep** — one structural bay, which is why the grid and the POS standard fit each other. If the condenser sits on the balcony, the area becomes **9.5 m²** (add 1.5 m²), which on the same 6.0 m bay means a depth of about 1.6 m is no longer enough; the depth stays at 1.8 m and the extra 1.5 m² is found by extending along the facade or by putting the condenser elsewhere (roof plant, or a dedicated services balcony).

Ground-level 25 m² courtyards are for any studios that land on the podium garden rather than on a typical floor. The brief’s **50% ground-level public open space** is communal / public, not D20 private open space — those are different tests and both apply.

| POS option | Minimum | Used on this project? |
|---|---|---|
| Ground courtyard | 25 m², dim. 3 m | Podium-edge studios only, if provided |
| Balcony, studio, other orientation | **8 m²**, dim. **1.8 m** | Typical floor — one 6.0 m bay × 1.8 m |
| Balcony, north | 8 m², dim. 1.7 m | North-facing studios |
| Balcony, south | 8 m², dim. 1.2 m | South-facing studios |
| + condenser on balcony | + **1.5 m²** | Either enlarge, or keep condensers off the balcony |
| Internal POS substitute | Table D9 at ≥ 40 m FFL | **Not available** at 19.2 m |

## 10. Clause 58.05-4 — Standard D21 Storage

> **58.05-4 Storage objective.** To provide adequate storage facilities for each dwelling.
>
> **Standard D21.** Each dwelling should have convenient access to **usable and secure** storage space. The total minimum storage space (including kitchen, bathroom and bedroom storage) should meet Table D10.
>
> **Table D10 (studio):** total **8 m³**, of which at least **5 m³** is **within the dwelling**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-4, Standard D21; Table D10 as in the *Apartment Design Guidelines for Victoria*.

Eight cubic metres for a studio, five of them inside the room, is a lot of volume in a 15–25 m² student studio that also has to fit a bed, a desk, an ensuite and a 1.0 m path to the door. A 600 mm-deep wardrobe 2.1 m high and 1.8 m wide is only about 2.3 m³. Kitchenette cupboards (if provided — Class 3 SOUs are not required to have cooking facilities, but many studios will have a bar fridge and a bench) and ensuite vanities add a little, not 5 m³. The honest reading is that **in-unit storage will fall short of 5 m³** unless the studio is at the top of the 15–25 m² range and the wardrobe is generous.

The remainder of the 8 m³ is the **basement or ground-floor storage cage** the brief already allows as optional basement program. A cage of about 3 m³ per room, lockable and on the same access-control system as the lobby, is the D21 backstop. That is a planning amenity, not an NCC requirement, and it should be drawn on the ground/basement plan rather than pretended to fit under the bed.

| Storage | Table D10 studio | Likely in a 15–25 m² room | Where the rest goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside the dwelling | ≥ **5 m³** | Wardrobe + ensuite + any kitchenette: typically **2–4 m³** | Flag the shortfall; enlarge wardrobe at the 25 m² end of the range |
| Total (in + out) | ≥ **8 m³** | — | Lockable cage in basement / ground, ~3–6 m³ |
| Bike storage | (separate brief item) | — | Secure bike store, not counted as D21 |

## 11. Clause 58.07-1 — Standard D26 Functional layout

> **58.07-1 Functional layout objective.** To ensure dwellings provide **functional areas** that meet the needs of residents.
>
> **Standard D26.** Bedrooms should meet the minimum internal room dimensions in Table D11, and provide an area **in addition** to those dimensions to accommodate a wardrobe. Living areas (excluding dining and kitchen) should meet Table D12.
>
> **Table D11:** main bedroom **3.0 m × 3.4 m**. Other bedrooms 3.0 m × 3.0 m.
>
> **Table D12:** studio and 1-bedroom living area **10 m²**, minimum dimension **3.3 m**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-1, Standard D26.

This is the sharpest conflict between Clause 58 and the locked brief. Table D11 plus Table D12 plus a wardrobe plus an ensuite is a **one-bedroom apartment**, not a 15–25 m² student studio. A 3.0 × 3.4 m bedroom is already 10.2 m²; a 10 m² living room with a 3.3 m minimum dimension is another 10 m²; ensuite, entry and wardrobe push the total well past 25 m². We are **not** secretly enlarging the AT1 studio to pass D26. The rooms remain 15–25 m² (30–35 m² DDA) on a 6.0 m bay, because that is the project.

What D26 still gives us, used as an amenity overlay rather than a re-planning of the brief, is a set of **minimum dimensions inside the studio**: a bed wall long enough for a 2.0 m bed plus bedside (the 3.4 m figure as an aspiration on the long side of the 6.0 m bay), a clear 3.0 m somewhere to sit and work, and a wardrobe that is *additional* to the sleeping rectangle rather than carved out of it. Combined kitchen/living/sleeping in one room is the Class 3 lodging model — residents eat in the 120–150 m² communal kitchen, so the Table D12 living-room area is partly provided in common space, not in the SOU. That is the argument to the objective; it is not a claim that the typical studio meets Table D11 and D12 as drawn for apartments.

| D26 table | Apartment figure | Student studio (locked) | How the objective is still addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main bedroom | 3.0 × 3.4 m + wardrobe | Sleeping zone inside 15–25 m² | 6.0 m bay supplies the long dimension |
| Living (studio / 1 bed) | 10 m², dim. 3.3 m | Combined live/sleep; no separate living room | Communal kitchen/dining 120–150 m² |
| DDA room | Same tables, plus circulation | 30–35 m² | Closest to a D26-compliant SOU |

## 12. Clause 58.07-2 — Standard D27 Room depth

> **58.07-2 Room depth objective.** To allow adequate **daylight** into single-aspect habitable rooms.
>
> **Standard D27.** Single-aspect habitable rooms should not exceed a room depth of **2.5 times the ceiling height**. The depth of a single-aspect, open-plan habitable room may be increased to **9 metres** if the room combines living, dining and kitchen, the kitchen is furthest from the window, and the ceiling height is at least **2.7 m**. Room depth is measured from the **external surface of the habitable-room window** to the rear wall.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-2, Standard D27.

This is the clause the course brief flags for written reflection, and it is the one the 6.0 m grid and the 2.7 m floor-to-ceiling height were already aiming at. **2.5 × 2.7 m = 6.75 m.** A studio that is one structural bay deep, measured from the outside of the facade glass to the corridor wall, sits at about **6.0 m** — inside 6.75 m, and well inside the 9 m open-plan concession. We do not need the 9 m concession. The kitchen-furthest-from-the-window rule is also the wrong picture: there is no in-board kitchen. The ensuite and wardrobe sit at the **corridor end** of the room (the darkest end), and the desk and bed sit toward the **window** — which is the D27 idea expressed in lodging rather than apartment furniture.

Ceiling height is a locked brief number: **FTC ≥ 2.7 m**. On a CLT floor with an acoustic topping of about 100 mm (STC 50) or about 195 mm (STC 58), that 2.7 m is measured to the finished ceiling, not to the underside of the raw CLT. Services over the ensuite can drop below 2.7 m; D27 already allows that over a kitchen, and the ensuite is the equivalent dark end of our room.

| Depth test | Limit | Typical studio on a 6.0 m grid |
|---|---|---|
| Single-aspect depth | **2.5 × FTC** = **6.75 m** at 2.7 m FTC | ~6.0 m glass to corridor wall |
| Open-plan concession | 9 m at ≥ 2.7 m FTC | Not needed |
| Dark end of the room | Kitchen furthest from the window | Ensuite + wardrobe at the corridor |
| FTC | ≥ 2.7 m | Locked brief; confirm after CLT acoustic buildup |

## 13. Clause 58.07-3 — Standard D28 Windows, and the 10% daylight test

> **58.07-3 Windows objective.** To allow adequate **daylight** into new habitable room windows.
>
> **Standard D28.** Habitable rooms should have a **window in an external wall** of the building. A window may provide daylight to a bedroom from a smaller secondary area within the bedroom where the window is clear to the sky. That secondary area should be at least **1.2 m** wide and not deeper than **1.5 times its width**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-3, Standard D28.
>
> **F6D7 — Natural lighting.** A habitable room must have a window, roof light or the like with an aggregate light-transmitting area of not less than **10% of the floor area** of the room.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. F6D7 (the quantitative daylight test the course brief pairs with D28).

D28 itself is a yes/no test: every habitable room looks at an external wall, not at a borrowed-light corridor. Our studios comply by construction — each room is a facade bay. There are no borrowed-light bedrooms and no light wells used as the only window. The 1.2 m secondary-area rule is for a bedroom that steps back from the facade; we do not need it if the glass sits on the external wall.

The number the course wants next to that clause is the **10% window-to-floor ratio**. For a 20 m² studio that is **2.0 m²** of glass. On a 6.0 m bay with 2.7 m floor-to-ceiling, a sliding door 2.4 m wide × 2.1 m high is already 5.0 m² — more than double the 10% test — and it is also the door onto the D20 balcony. A smaller punched window of 1.8 × 1.2 m is only 2.16 m², which still passes 10% on a 20 m² room but fails it on a 25 m² room. The safe drawing rule is: **the balcony door is the daylight window**, sized so glass area ≥ 10% of the room, on the external wall, with no habitable room looking only at the corridor.

| Daylight test | Requirement | Typical 20 m² studio |
|---|---|---|
| D28 — window in an external wall | Yes / no | Yes — one 6.0 m facade bay |
| Borrowed light / light well as only window | Not accepted as the D28 window | Not used |
| F6D7 / brief — glass ≥ 10% floor | 2.0 m² on a 20 m² room; 2.5 m² on a 25 m² room | Balcony sliding door ~2.4 × 2.1 m = 5.0 m² |
| Secondary daylight area | 1.2 m wide, depth ≤ 1.5 × width | Not required if glass is on the facade |

## 14. Clause 58.07-4 — Standard D29 Natural ventilation, and where this lands on the drawings

> **58.07-4 Natural ventilation objectives.** To encourage **natural ventilation** of dwellings. To allow occupants to effectively manage natural ventilation of dwellings.
>
> **Standard D29.** The design should maximise **openable windows, doors or other ventilation devices** in external walls. At least **40 per cent of dwellings** should provide effective **cross ventilation** with a breeze path of **5–18 m** between openings of approximately the same area, measured between ventilation openings on **different orientations**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-4, Standard D29.

A double-loaded corridor on a 6.0 m grid produces **single-aspect** studios: one window wall, then the room, then the corridor. That plate cannot put 40% of rooms on two orientations without breaking the AT1 typical floor. Corner rooms at the ends of the building can be dual-aspect; a 20-room floor might yield four corners — 20%, not 40%. The remaining rooms ventilate through the balcony door (D28’s window, now openable) and, where the brief allows it, **air-conditioning**. AT2.3 will take that mechanical system under J6. D29’s objective — occupants able to manage ventilation — is still met by a full-height openable balcony door on every studio, even where a second orientation is not available.

Cross-ventilation that *does* exist on this project sits in the **communal** rooms: the 120–150 m² kitchen/dining can be dual-aspect or through-ventilated; level lounges at 20 m² can sit on corners. Those rooms are not “dwellings” for the 40% count, but they are where students spend waking hours, and they are the honest place to show a 5–18 m breeze path.

| Ventilation | D29 | This typical floor |
|---|---|---|
| Openable device in an external wall | Maximise | Balcony sliding door to every studio |
| Dual-aspect / cross-vent dwellings | ≥ **40%**, breeze path 5–18 m | Corner rooms only — **below 40%**; stated as a variation |
| Mechanical fallback | Brief allows AC | Per-room AC + corridor ventilation; see AT2.3 J6D3 / J6D4 |
| Communal cross-vent | (not in the 40% count) | Kitchen/dining and end lounges |
