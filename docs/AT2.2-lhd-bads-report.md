## 1. NCC Part G7 — Livable housing design

> **G7P1 — Livable housing design.** Each sole-occupancy unit in a Class 2 building must be provided with at least one **level and step-free entrance door** from an accessible part of the floor; **internal doors and corridors** which facilitate unimpeded movement; a **sanitary compartment** that facilitates independent access and use, on the **entry level**; a **shower** that facilitates independent access and use; and walls constructed so as to facilitate **future installation of grabrails** without removing existing linings.
>
> **Applications:** G7P1(a) only applies to a sole-occupancy unit on an **accessible floor**. G7O1 / G7P1 only apply to a **Class 2 sole-occupancy unit**.
>
> **G7D2.** Each sole-occupancy unit in a Class 2 building must comply with the **ABCB Standard for Livable Housing Design**, **except for Part 1** (the street-to-door path — already covered by Part D4 and the Premises Standards).
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. G7P1, G7D2.

The course asks for Part G7 on a Class 2 building. Ours is **Class 3 lodging** (AT2.1 §1, A6G4): unrelated students, shared 120–150 m² kitchen, managed occupancy. We are **not** reclassifying to make G7 a Deemed-to-Satisfy pathway. **Clause 58** still attaches because the site is **ACZ1** (AT1.2) — that is a planning trigger, not a building-class trigger. G7 is applied as the amenity overlay the brief already asked for (“LHD where possible”) on every studio.

Every G7P1 item has a counterpart on the typical floor: lift-served corridor, no step at the unit door; studio door + ensuite door on a 3.6 m bay; ensuite on the same level; shower in that ensuite; noggings in the ensuite stud lining (not in the CLT panel). G7D2 skips LHD Part 1; our street-to-lift path is already AT2.1 §6 (AS 1428.1, **850 mm** clear, **1540 mm** turning). Parts 2–6 of the Standard are the overlay; construction numbers are on slides 10–11.

**On the real drawing:** A102 and A103 already show the room outlines and door swings for every studio and ensuite on the typical floor, at the correct 3.6 m bay width — the geometry this slide's numbers depend on is genuinely modelled, not sketched. What the sheets do not yet carry is a door schedule: no leaf width or clear-opening figure is tagged against any of those door symbols, and the ensuite walls are not called out as a distinct wall type from the CLT structure around them. That distinction matters directly for this clause, because the 820 mm/850 mm test and the noggings requirement on slide 11 both depend on the ensuite wall actually being stud construction rather than CLT — a fact the plan implies through its wall thickness but does not yet state on the drawing itself. A door schedule and a wall-type legend are both standard Revit outputs once the model is tagged, and would turn this slide from an argument about the design into a verified reading of the drawing set.

| Clause 58 / BADS | Site is **ACZ1** | D18–D21 and D26–D29 on the studio module |

## 2. Clause 58.05-1 — Standard D18 Accessibility

> **58.05-1 Accessibility objective.** To ensure the design of dwellings meets the needs of people with **limited mobility**.
>
> **Standard D18.** At least **50 per cent of dwellings** should have: a clear opening width of at least **850 mm** at the entrance and the main bedroom; a clear path of **1.2 metres** connecting entrance, main bedroom, adaptable bathroom and living area; a main bedroom with access to an adaptable bathroom; and at least one **adaptable bathroom** meeting Design A or Design B in Table D7.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-1, Standard D18.

D18 is stricter than LHD Silver: 850 mm doors and a Table D7 bathroom in **half of all dwellings**. Our brief’s DDA ratio is **1 in 20** (5%) at 30–35 m². That 5% meets Design A/B and AS 1428.1. It does **not** meet D18’s 50%. A 15–25 m² studio cannot hold a Table D7 bathroom, a 1.2 m path and a 3.0 × 3.4 m bedroom. The response is layered: **every studio** gets the LHD overlay; **one in twenty**, on the same 3.6 × 7.2 m bay grid, is a DDA room; the **common path** (street, lobby, lift, corridor) is fully accessible. The 50% adaptable-apartment ratio is a Class 2 test this floor plate does not claim to pass.

**On the real drawing:** A501's unit schedule already carries the right structure for this test. It lists 5 units under "Unit Accessible Typology" against 195 under "Unit Typology A" — a genuine 1-in-20 ratio built into the model, not a number asserted in text without a corresponding room type. That is the harder half of D18 to get right, and it is already done. The half that is not done: every row in that schedule currently shows its Area column as "Not Placed," meaning Revit has not yet calculated or had entered against it the floor area of a single unit, accessible or typical. Until that column is populated, the 30–35 m² DDA figure and the 15–25 m² typical range used throughout this report are both design targets carried over from the brief, not measurements taken from the model — a distinction worth being honest about on a slide that otherwise reads as verified.

| D18 test | Standard | This project |
|---|---|---|
| Share of dwellings | 50% adaptable | 5% DDA (brief 1:20) + 100% LHD overlay |
| Entrance / bedroom door | 850 mm | 850 mm on DDA + accessible path; 820 mm LHD on typical studios |
| Internal path | 1.2 m | 1.2 m in DDA rooms; 1.0 m LHD in typical studios |
| Adaptable bathroom | Table D7 Design A or B | DDA ensuites only |

## 3. Clause 58.05-2 — Standard D19 Building entry and circulation

> **58.05-2 Building entry and circulation objectives.** To provide each dwelling and building with its own **sense of identity**. To ensure **safe, functional and efficient movement** of residents. To ensure internal communal areas have **daylight and natural ventilation**.
>
> **Standard D19.** Entries should be **visible and easily identifiable**, and provide **shelter**, personal address and a **transitional space**. The layout should **clearly distinguish** residential and non-residential entrances; provide **windows to building entrances and lift areas**; provide visible, safe stairs from the entry level; and provide corridors with **natural light and ventilation**, no obstruction from services, and **clear sight lines**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-2, Standard D19.

The same circulation AT2.1 measured for escape, now read as amenity. Two identities at the street: **Class 6 retail** on Nicholson Street, and a separate **residential lobby**. D19 is the planning reason those doors are not interchangeable. The two fire-isolated stairs in the RC core should be **visible from the entry level** so residents use them — a glazed screen or daylit lift lobby. The corridor is 1.8–2.0 m, risers in the core, sight lines from the lift to both stairs (~24–30 m, inside D2D6’s 9–45 m). Mid-block daylight on a double-loaded ~20-room floor is the hard D19 test: end windows, a glazed lobby, or mechanical air. A 3.6 m-bay plate will not have a window in the middle of the corridor.

**On the real drawing:** A101 (ground floor plan) and A503 (means of escape) both already show the two things D19 tests most concretely — a residential lobby drawn as a distinct space from the retail frontage, and both fire-isolated stairs in their real position in the RC core. The identity split and the stair count are facts about the current model, not a description of intent. What neither sheet does yet is tell you whether a resident standing in that lobby can actually *see* either stair door — no glazing is called out on the lift-lobby wall, and no sightline or angle has been checked from the entry to the stair doors. That is the part of D19 ("provide visible, safe stairs from the entry level") this slide cannot yet claim is met, as distinct from the parts (lobby identity, stair position) it can.

|---|---|
| Identifiable entry | Dedicated residential lobby, separate from retail |
| Distinguish residential / non-residential | Class 6 shopfront vs Class 3 lobby |
| Windows to entries and lift | Glazed lift lobby at the RC core |
| Visible stairs | Both core stairs readable from the lobby |
| Corridor light, air, sight lines | 1.8–2.0 m clear; risers in the core; end windows or mechanical air |

## 4. Clause 58.05-3 — Standard D20 Private open space

> **58.05-3 Private open space objective.** To provide adequate private open space for the reasonable recreation and service needs of residents.
>
> **Standard D20.** A dwelling should have at least one of: ground-level **25 m²** (dimension **3 m**); a **balcony** per Table D8; podium **15 m²** (dimension **3 m**); or roof **10 m²** (dimension **2 m**) — each with access from a living room. If a cooling or heating unit is on a balcony, add **1.5 m²**. If FFL is **40 m or more**, Table D8 does not apply if Table D9 extra internal area is provided.
>
> **Table D8 — studio / 1 bedroom, other orientation:** **8 m²**, minimum dimension **1.8 m**. North: 8 m² / 1.7 m. South: 8 m² / 1.2 m.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-3, Standard D20.

The site elevation (A200/A201) carries two sub-precinct envelopes, not one: Schedule 1B (**19.2 m / 6 storeys**) at the street frontage, and Schedule 1D (**31 m / 10 storeys**, with a 5 m setback above 5 storeys) behind it. The built massing runs to **31.0 m** (FFL 17.740 to 48.740, 9 residential levels + roof) — still under **40 m**, so Table D9 internal POS is **not** available anywhere on this building. Every studio needs a balcony.

This is where the real 3.6 m bay changes the answer. Table D8’s **8 m²** at **1.8 m** depth needs a facade width of 8 ÷ 1.8 = **4.4 m** — wider than our **3.6 m** studio bay. A straight 1.8 m-deep balcony on one bay is only 3.6 × 1.8 = **6.48 m²**, short of 8 m². Two honest fixes, either drawable in AT2.4: **(a)** deepen the balcony to **~2.25 m** (3.6 × 2.25 = 8.1 m²) — a bigger cantilever off the 7.2 m beam line, feeding the AT2.3 thermal-break detail; or **(b)** let the balcony run past the party line into a shared notch between two bays. If the condenser sits on the balcony, add **1.5 m²** on top of whichever fix is used. The brief’s **50% ground-level public open space** is communal / public, not D20 private open space — both apply.

**On the real drawing:** the balcony line itself is modelled — A102 and A103 both show it running the width of every studio bay — but at 1:200 that line carries no printed depth, and neither elevation (A200, A201) dimensions it either. So the 6.48 m² failing figure and the ~2.25 m fix above are both this report's own calculation against the 3.6 m bay width, not a number read off the drawing. Until a depth dimension actually appears on A102/A103 or A200/A201, this slide is making the case for what the balcony depth needs to be, not confirming what it already is — worth stating plainly rather than implying the fix is already drawn.

| POS option | Minimum | Used here? |
|---|---|---|
| Ground courtyard | 25 m², dim. 3 m | Podium-edge studios only, if provided |
| Balcony, studio, other orientation | **8 m²**, dim. **1.8 m** | 3.6 m bay × 1.8 m = 6.48 m² — **short**; needs ~2.25 m depth |
| + condenser on balcony | + **1.5 m²** | Add to whichever depth fix is used |
| Internal POS substitute | Table D9 at ≥ 40 m FFL | **Not available** — built height 31 m, still < 40 m |

## 5. Clause 58.05-4 — Standard D21 Storage

> **58.05-4 Storage objective.** To provide adequate storage facilities for each dwelling.
>
> **Standard D21.** Each dwelling should have convenient access to **usable and secure** storage. Total minimum storage (including kitchen, bathroom and bedroom storage) should meet Table D10.
>
> **Table D10 (studio):** total **8 m³**, of which at least **5 m³** is **within the dwelling**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.05-4, Standard D21; Table D10 as in the *Apartment Design Guidelines for Victoria*.

Five cubic metres inside a 15–25 m² studio that also holds a bed, desk, ensuite and a 1.0 m path is tight. A 600 mm-deep wardrobe 2.1 m high × 1.8 m wide is about 2.3 m³. **In-unit storage will typically fall short of 5 m³** unless the studio is at the top of the range and the wardrobe is generous. The rest of the 8 m³ is a **lockable cage** in the basement or at ground (~3–6 m³), which the brief already allows as optional basement program. Bike storage is a separate brief item, not D21.

**On the real drawing:** A501's furniture list for Unit Typology A names a storage cupboard as the first item in the room — in-unit storage is a real, modelled piece of furniture in this design, not an assumption invented to answer D21. It is not, however, a dimensioned object: no width, depth or height is attached to it, which is why the 2–3 m³ estimate above is this report's own reasonable guess at a 600 mm wardrobe, not a figure read from the schedule. The basement plan (A100) is further behind again — it shows no cage or storage zone at all yet, so the "rest of the 8 m³ goes to a lockable cage" line in the table above describes where the brief allows that shortfall to be resolved, not something already resolved on a sheet.

| Storage | Table D10 studio | Likely in 15–25 m² | Where the rest goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside the dwelling | ≥ **5 m³** | Typically **2–4 m³** | Flag the shortfall; enlarge wardrobe at 25 m² |
| Total (in + out) | ≥ **8 m³** | — | Lockable cage, basement / ground |
| Bike storage | — | — | Secure bike store, not counted as D21 |

> **58.07-1 Functional layout objective.** To ensure dwellings provide **functional areas** that meet the needs of residents.

> **Standard D26.** Bedrooms should meet Table D11, plus extra area for a wardrobe. Living areas (excluding dining and kitchen) should meet Table D12.
>
> **Table D11:** main bedroom **3.0 m × 3.4 m**. **Table D12:** studio / 1-bedroom living **10 m²**, minimum dimension **3.3 m**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-1, Standard D26.

This is the sharpest conflict with the locked brief. ## 7. Clause 58.07-2 — Standard D27 Room depth

> **58.07-2 Room depth objective.** To allow adequate **daylight** into single-aspect habitable rooms.
>
> **Standard D27.** Single-aspect habitable rooms should not exceed a room depth of **2.5 times the ceiling height**. Open-plan depth may increase to **9 metres** if living, dining and kitchen are combined, the kitchen is furthest from the window, and ceiling height is at least **2.7 m**. Measure from the **external surface of the habitable-room window** to the rear wall.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-2, Standard D27.

This is the clause the course flags for written reflection, and the Week 6 Revit model changes the answer. **2.5 × 2.7 m = 6.75 m.** Unit Typology A (A501) sits on **two 3.6 m modules deep = 7.2 m**, glass to corridor wall — **over** 6.75 m, not under it as earlier drafts assumed on a 6.0 m guess. Measured strictly, a full-depth single-aspect studio **fails D27** on its own.

Two honest responses, not a redraw of the locked module: **(1)** D27 measures “habitable room” depth from the external window to the *rear wall of that room* — if the ensuite and wardrobe are boxed off as a separate enclosed zone at the corridor end (as A501’s Unit Typology A plan already shows: ensuite as its own room, not an open recess), the **habitable** portion of the room is shorter than the full 7.2 m structural bay, and can be brought under 6.75 m by holding the ensuite/wardrobe box to ≥ 0.5 m deep. **(2)** if the habitable zone still runs close to 7.2 m, invoke the **9 m open-plan concession** — it requires kitchen, living and dining combined with the kitchen furthest from the window; our studios have no kitchen (it is communal — residents eat in the shared 120–150 m² kitchen/dining, not in the room), so this concession does not cleanly apply and should not be claimed without a tutor check. Route (1) is the one to draw in AT2.4; route (2) is flagged here as a real, unresolved risk carried forward — not quietly assumed compliant.

**On the real drawing:** this is the one place where the model already agrees with the fix this report is arguing for. A501's Unit Typology A plan draws the ensuite as its own enclosed room at the corridor end of the unit, not as an open recess off the main space — exactly the configuration fix (1) above needs to exist. What the drawing does not yet do is dimension how deep that box actually is. Until it carries a printed depth, "≥ 0.5 m" is the minimum this report calculates is needed to bring the habitable zone under 6.75 m, not a number confirmed on the sheet — meaning D27 currently sits as a real, open compliance question rather than a solved one, even though the room layout that would solve it is already drawn.

| Depth test | Limit | Typical studio, real 3.6 × 7.2 m bay |
|---|---|---|
| Single-aspect depth | **2.5 × FTC** = **6.75 m** at 2.7 m | **7.2 m** full bay depth — **exceeds the limit** |
| Fix (1): habitable zone only | Must be ≤ 6.75 m | Box off ensuite/wardrobe ≥ 0.5 m deep at the corridor end |
| Fix (2): open-plan concession | 9 m at ≥ 2.7 m FTC, kitchen furthest from window | Does not cleanly apply — no in-room kitchen; unresolved risk |
| Dark end of the room | Kitchen furthest from the window (concession) / corridor end (fix 1) | Ensuite + wardrobe at the corridor |
| FTC | ≥ 2.7 m | Locked brief; confirm after acoustic buildup |

## 8. Clause 58.07-3 — Standard D28 Windows

> **58.07-3 Windows objective.** To allow adequate **daylight** into new habitable room windows.
>
> **Standard D28.** Habitable rooms should have a **window in an external wall**. A window may serve a bedroom from a smaller secondary area, clear to the sky, at least **1.2 m** wide and not deeper than **1.5 times its width**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-3, Standard D28.
>
> **F6D7 — Natural lighting.** A habitable room must have a window or roof light with an aggregate light-transmitting area of not less than **10% of the floor area** of the room.
>
> — ABCB, *NCC 2022*, Volume One, cl. F6D7.

D28 is a yes/no test: every habitable room looks at an external wall. Each studio is a facade bay — no borrowed-light bedrooms. The 10% window-to-floor ratio is the number next to that clause: **2.0 m²** of glass on a 20 m² studio, **2.5 m²** on 25 m². On the real **3.6 m** bay with 2.7 m FTC, a sliding door 2.4 m × 2.1 m is 5.0 m² — also the D20 balcony door, and it fits comfortably inside the narrower 3.6 m frontage. A 1.8 × 1.2 m punched window (2.16 m²) passes 10% on 20 m² and fails on 25 m². Drawing rule: **the balcony door is the daylight window**, glass ≥ 10% of the room, on the external wall.

**On the real drawing:** 2.4 × 2.1 m is a standard sliding-door size chosen to fit comfortably inside the real 3.6 m bay, not a dimension pulled from a schedule on A102 or A103 — neither sheet currently tags door or window sizes at all. That makes the 5.0 m² glass area, and the "passes 10% comfortably" conclusion that follows from it, a reasonable and checkable proposal rather than a confirmed fact. Since 5.0 m² clears the 2.0–2.5 m² requirement by roughly double, a modest error in the assumed door size would not change the pass — which is exactly the kind of margin worth stating on the slide, rather than either overclaiming certainty or hedging on a result that is not actually in doubt.

| Daylight test | Requirement | Typical 20 m² studio |
|---|---|---|
| D28 — window in an external wall | Yes / no | Yes — one 3.6 m facade bay |
| F6D7 — glass ≥ 10% floor | 2.0 m² (20 m² room); 2.5 m² (25 m²) | Balcony door ~2.4 × 2.1 m = 5.0 m² |
| Secondary daylight area | 1.2 m wide, depth ≤ 1.5 × width | Not required if glass is on the facade |

## 9. Clause 58.07-4 — Standard D29 Natural ventilation

> **58.07-4 Natural ventilation objectives.** To encourage **natural ventilation** of dwellings and allow occupants to manage it.
>
> **Standard D29.** Maximise **openable windows, doors or other ventilation devices** in external walls. At least **40 per cent of dwellings** should provide effective **cross ventilation** with a breeze path of **5–18 m** between openings of approximately the same area, on **different orientations**.
>
> — *Maribyrnong Planning Scheme*, cl. 58.07-4, Standard D29.

A double-loaded corridor on a 3.6 m-bay grid produces **single-aspect** studios. That plate cannot put 40% of rooms on two orientations without breaking the AT1 typical floor. Four corners on a 20-room floor is 20%, not 40%. Remaining rooms ventilate through the openable balcony door and, where the brief allows it, **air-conditioning** (AT2.3, J6). The objective — occupants able to manage ventilation — is still met by a full-height openable door on every studio. Cross-ventilation that *does* exist sits in **communal** rooms (kitchen/dining, end lounges), where students spend waking hours, even though those rooms are not in the 40% dwelling count.

**On the real drawing:** the building genuinely has four corners, and each one is a real dual-aspect opportunity on A102/A103's actual footprint — this is not a hypothetical count. What is not yet on the drawing is any tag, hatch or callout identifying which specific rooms occupy those corners, as distinct from the single-aspect rooms along the straight runs of corridor between them. That is a small drafting step (four rooms, one highlight colour) that would turn "four corners on a ~20-room floor is 20%" from a claim this report makes about the plan's geometry into something a tutor can verify by looking at the sheet directly.

|---|---|---|
| Openable device in an external wall | Maximise | Balcony sliding door to every studio |
| Dual-aspect / cross-vent dwellings | ≥ **40%**, breeze path 5–18 m | Corner rooms only — **below 40%**; stated as a variation |
| Mechanical fallback | Brief allows AC | Per-room AC; see AT2.3 J6D3 / J6D4 |

## 10. LHD doors, corridors and thresholds

> **Clause 2.1 / 3.1.** Entrance and internal doors: minimum clear opening **820 mm** (typically an **870 mm** leaf).
>
> **Clause 3.3.** Corridors connected to those doors: minimum clear width **1000 mm**.
>
> **Clause 2.2 / 3.2.** Threshold **level**, or sill **≤ 5 mm** if rounded or bevelled, or ramped not steeper than **1:8**.
>
> — ABCB, *Livable Housing Design Standard 2022* (v1.3), Parts 2 and 3.

This is the door-width reflection the course asks for. LHD Silver is 820 mm. On the **accessible path and every DDA studio**, AT2.1 already uses the stricter AS 1428.1 **850 mm** — wider than LHD, not narrower. Standard studios: 820 mm at unit door and ensuite door, flush or ≤ 5 mm sill into the CLT acoustic topping. There is no house-style hallway inside a 15–25 m² studio. The 1000 mm test lands on the **common corridor** (already 1.8–2.0 m) and the **clear strip past bed and wardrobe** to the ensuite, which the 3.6 × 7.2 m bay can hold without enlarging the AT1 module.

**On the real drawing:** the corridor loop is drawn at what reads, by eye against the 3.6 m bay grid, as roughly the 1.8–2.0 m this slide claims — but that is a visual estimate against the grid, not a printed dimension string on A102 or A103. Door widths sit in the same position: the door symbols are there, swung the right way, but nothing on either sheet yet states 820 mm or 850 mm as a number. Both gaps are the same door-schedule gap named on slide 1, showing up again here because it affects two different clauses (LHD Part 3's corridor width and this project's own DDA door width) from the same missing piece of documentation.

| Door / path | LHD minimum | What we provide |
|---|---|---|
| Studio entrance, standard | 820 mm clear | 820 mm (870 mm leaf) |
| DDA / accessible path | 820 mm LHD / **850 mm** AS 1428.1 | **850 mm** — the stricter figure |
| Ensuite door | 820 mm | 820 mm standard; 850 mm in DDA rooms |

## 11. LHD sanitary, hobless shower and noggings

> **Clause 4.2.** Sanitary compartment: **900 mm** between walls either side of the pan; basin **≥ 450 mm** from the pan centreline; clear **1200 mm × 900 mm** in front of the pan, **clear of the door swing**.
>
> **Clause 5.2.** At least one shower **hobless and step-free**; lip **≤ 5 mm**.
>
> **Clause 6.2.** Wall reinforcing: **12 mm structural plywood** or **timber noggings ≥ 25 mm**, typically **600–1600 mm** above finished floor — unless the wall is concrete or masonry.
>
> — ABCB, *Livable Housing Design Standard 2022* (v1.3), Parts 4–6.

Every studio is single-level, so the WC in the ensuite meets “entry level.” LHD circulation is smaller than AS 1428.1’s 1540 mm turning circle — that is why DDA rooms are 30–35 m². Standard ensuites use a cavity slider or outward door so the leaf does not eat the 1200 × 900 mm zone. Hobless and step-free are both required. DDA ensuites run the AT2.1 Spec 26 membrane across the whole floor; standard studios repeat the same hobless detail. Ensuite walls are stud lining on CLT, **not** masonry, so noggings are required. Grabrails are **not** fitted now — only blocking.

**On the real drawing:** every ensuite in the model is a genuine enclosed room, drawn consistently across A102, A103, and A501's unit typology plans — this is not a detail floating separately from the design. But every one of those sheets sits at 1:100 or 1:200, a scale that can show a fixture layout but cannot show a 1200 × 900 mm clear zone, a hobless threshold detail, or a noggings zone meaningfully. A501 already goes further than the floor plans by drawing an axonometric of the unit — the natural next step, not yet taken, is a 1:20 ensuite detail in the same spirit, which is the scale these three LHD requirements actually need to be checked at.

| Element | LHD Silver (every studio) | AS 1428.1 (DDA only) |
|---|---|---|
| Clear zone in front of pan | 1200 × 900 mm, clear of the door | 1540 mm turning circle |
| Shower | Hobless **and** step-free; lip ≤ 5 mm | Same, full-floor membrane |
| Noggings | 12 mm plywood or 25 mm, ~600–1600 mm AFFL | Same stud walls; exemption does not apply |
