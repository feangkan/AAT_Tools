# AT1.4 Mass Timber Connector Strategy

**Project:** 200-bed mixed-use PBSA at 63–67 Nicholson St, Footscray  
**Resource:** WoodWorks Index of Mass Timber Connections (May 2024)  
**Scope:** Which connectors benefit the AT1.4 structural design

---

## Project Context

**Building Type:**
- Class 3 (PBSA) — NCC compliant
- Podium + Tower (mixed-use strategy)
- Ground floor: Retail (likely concrete/steel)
- Levels 1–2: Community spaces
- Levels 3+: Student residential (CLT/mass timber opportunity)

**Structural Opportunity:**
- CLT floor panels + wall panels (residential levels)
- Concrete podium (ground + community)
- Modular room grid (3.2–3.6m × 6–7m typical)
- Mechanical system: Clean vertical cores

**Key Constraints:**
- Fire rating: NCC Class 3 (FRL requirements)
- Vibration control: Multi-occupant residential
- Efficiency: 70–80% net usable (modular repetition)
- Accessibility: AS 1428.1 compliance

## Connector Categories Beneficial to AT1.4

### **TIER 1: MOST CRITICAL (Podium-to-Residential Interface)**

These connectors form the structural transition from concrete podium to mass timber residential levels.

#### **Table 5: Panel Support at Concrete or Masonry** ⭐⭐⭐

**Why Critical for AT1.4:**
- Retail/community floors sit on concrete podium
- First residential level (Level 3) sits on top of podium
- Transition between different materials (concrete → CLT)
- Accommodation for tolerance differences (concrete ±25mm vs. timber ±6mm)

**Recommended Connectors:**

**5-1. Panel Bears at Top of Wall (HIGH CAPACITY, Level II-III Fire Rating)**
- **Best for:** Ground-level floor panels on podium edge
- **Load:** High vertical capacity
- **Cost:** $$
- **Fire Rating:** Level II–III (can be fire-rated)
- **Benefits:** 
  - Grout below panel absorbs elevation differences
  - Bearing plate interface manages concrete/timber tolerance
  - Easy inspection before panel installation
- **AT1.4 Application:** Level 3 floor panels on top of podium concrete

**5-3. Panel Bears on Ledger at Wall (HIGH CAPACITY, Level II-III Fire Rating)**
- **Best for:** Continuous ledger running along podium perimeter
- **Load:** High vertical capacity
- **Cost:** $$
- **Fire Rating:** Level II–III
- **Benefits:**
  - Supports multiple levels of residential panels
  - Clear load path
  - Repeatable detail
- **AT1.4 Application:** Continuous support for all residential level floor panels (Levels 3+)

**5-5. Panel Supported by Proprietary Connector/Holdown**
- **Best for:** Lateral load transfer (diaphragm to core)
- **Load:** Medium (lateral focus)
- **Cost:** $$
- **Fire Rating:** Level III
- **Benefits:**
  - Transfers earthquake/wind loads from CLT diaphragm into concrete core
  - Doesn't interfere with gravity load path
- **AT1.4 Application:** Drag strut connections linking floor plates to lateral-resistance cores

### **TIER 2: RESIDENTIAL FLOOR ASSEMBLY (Panel-to-Panel)**

#### **Table 1: Mass Timber Panel to Panel** ⭐⭐

These manage CLT floor panel-to-panel connections within the residential floor plate.

**1-1. Panels Connect with Single Surface Spline (MOST ECONOMICAL)**
- **Best for:** Typical floor-to-floor panel joints
- **Load:** Medium (controlled shear)
- **Cost:** $ (cheapest option)
- **Fire Rating:** Level I (inherently fire-rated)
- **Constructability:** Easy (screws/nails + plywood spline)
- **Benefits:**
  - Simple, repeatable detail
  - Works with modular grid (panel joints align with module lines)
  - Fire-rated without extra work
  - Minimal acoustic issues if panel tightness maintained
- **AT1.4 Application:** Standard floor-to-floor panel joints for all residential levels
- **Economics:** With 200 beds × 4 floors = ~100+ panel joints; economical detail = major cost savings

**1-3. Panels Connect with Steel Surface Spline (HIGHER CAPACITY)**
- **Best for:** Where 1-1 capacity insufficient or acoustic separation critical
- **Load:** High (steel provides added stiffness)
- **Cost:** $$ (more expensive spline)
- **Fire Rating:** Level III (requires protection)
- **Constructability:** Moderate (steel spline handling)
- **Benefits:**
  - Higher capacity for wider spans
  - Steel stiffness reduces deflection (vibration control)
  - Good for acoustic isolation (metal breaks acoustic bridge)
- **AT1.4 Application:** Consider for larger open community spaces (dining, lounges) if span > 8m

**1-2. NLT or DLT Panels Connect with Single Surface Spline**
- **Best for:** If using NLT/DLT instead of CLT (lower cost, similar performance)
- **Load:** Medium
- **Cost:** $
- **Fire Rating:** Level I
- **Notes:** Requires coordination with panel supplier (sheathing hold-back pre-manufactured)

### **TIER 3: VERTICAL SUPPORT (Walls & Columns)**

#### **Table 3: Panel Point Support at Column** ⭐⭐

For CLT columns supporting floor panels (two-way spanning design).

**3-1. Panel Bears on Column (SIMPLEST)**
- **Best for:** Interior column supports (low cost, clean look)
- **Load:** Medium (perpendicular-to-grain bearing)
- **Cost:** $ (screws only)
- **Fire Rating:** Level II
- **Constructability:** Easy
- **Benefits:**
  - Panel continuous over column (fewer joints)
  - Clean underside (no brackets visible)
  - Minimal coordination
- **AT1.4 Application:** Interior columns in residential levels if using post-and-beam hybrid

**3-4. Floor Panel Bears on Column at Proprietary Standoff (ADVANCED)**
- **Best for:** Stacked columns (multi-story alignment, eccentricity control)
- **Load:** Medium (controlled by manufacturer)
- **Cost:** $$$ (proprietary connector)
- **Fire Rating:** Level III
- **Benefits:**
  - Pre-installed on column bottoms
  - Allows for column stacking without drift
  - Better control of multi-story alignment
- **AT1.4 Application:** If using stacked CLT columns through multiple floors

#### **Table 4: Panel Support at Wall Panel** ⭐⭐⭐

Floor panels bearing on mass timber wall panels (common in CLT structures).

**4-1. Floor Panel Bears on Wall – Platform (MOST ECONOMICAL)**
- **Best for:** Typical floor-on-wall connection
- **Load:** Medium (bearing capacity of wall face)
- **Cost:** $ (screws only)
- **Fire Rating:** Level II
- **Constructability:** Easy (diagonal screws for top wall attachment)
- **Benefits:**
  - Works with wall panels oriented vertically
  - Simple screw attachment
  - Inherently fire-rated
  - Repeatable detail across all levels
- **AT1.4 Application:** Standard connection at building perimeter and any interior mass timber walls

**4-6. Floor Panel Bears on Wall – Platform with Concealed Plates**
- **Best for:** Where secondary lateral load capacity required
- **Load:** Medium (with secondary out-of-plane capacity)
- **Cost:** $$$
- **Fire Rating:** Level II
- **Constructability:** Advanced (pre-installation of concealed connectors)
- **Benefits:**
  - Handles out-of-plane wall loads (wind, seismic)
  - Concealed (clean appearance)
  - Fire-rated by design
- **AT1.4 Application:** Consider if significant lateral loads anticipated (wind exposure on podium corners)

### **TIER 4: FOUNDATION & BASE CONNECTIONS**

#### **Table 16: Wall Panel Base at Concrete** ⭐⭐⭐

Ground-floor mass timber walls on concrete podium.

**16-1. Panel Bears with Sill Plate (MOST COMMON)**
- **Best for:** Wall panel base on concrete podium
- **Load:** High (concentrated wall loads)
- **Cost:** $$
- **Fire Rating:** Level II–III
- **Benefits:**
  - Pressure-treated wood sill manages moisture
  - Dowel connections to concrete foundation
  - Clear load transfer
- **AT1.4 Application:** Base of any CLT walls on podium (e.g., service walls, acoustic buffer)

**16-8. Panel Holdown with Rod System**
- **Best for:** Multi-story holdown (preventing uplift in tall panels)
- **Load:** Medium–High (tension-controlled)
- **Cost:** $$
- **Fire Rating:** Level III
- **Benefits:**
  - Rod system through multiple stories
  - Handles seismic overturning
  - Symmetric load path
- **AT1.4 Application:** Acoustic wall core details (internal service walls)

### **TIER 5: SECONDARY (Nice-to-Have, Not Essential)**

#### **Table 2: Panel Support at Beam** ⭐

**2-1. Panel Bears on Beam (LOW COST)**

## Connector Optimization Strategy for AT1.4

### **Repeatability & Cost Control**

The key to economical mass timber construction is **connector repetition**. Target these four details:

| Connector | Application | Frequency | Cost | Fire Rating |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|------|------------|
| **Table 5-3** | Podium-to-Level 3 floor | 1× around perimeter | $$ | Level II–III |
| **Table 1-1** | Floor-to-floor panel joints | 100+ across all levels | $ | Level I |
| **Table 4-1** | Floor-on-wall at perimeter | 40+ (2 per floor × 20 floors) | $ | Level II |
| **Table 16-1** | Wall base on concrete | As needed for interior walls | $$ | Level II–III |

**Estimated savings from repetition:** If 80% of connections use economical Class 1 details (splines, simple bearing, screws), structural connection cost reduction: **15–20%**.

### **Fire-Rating Strategy**

NCC Class 3 requires FRL 60/60/60 minimum. Strategy:

1. **Rely on inherent fire resistance:** CLT thickness + spline details (Table 1-1, Table 4-1) often meet fire rating without added protection.
2. **Test early:** Consult fire engineer to verify which simple details achieve required rating; avoid costly proprietary connectors if standard details work.
3. **Concealment where needed:** For Table 4-6 (concealed plates), fire rating is built-in via design, not additional material.

## Key Design Notes from WoodWorks Index

### **Moisture & Shrinkage (Critical for Residential)**

**Issue:** CLT shrinks perpendicular-to-grain (0.5–1% moisture change). Residential buildings are conditioned; long-term moisture cycling causes settlement.

**Mitigation:**
- **Slightly countersink screws** in floor panels to account for long-term shrinkage (see WoodWorks 5-3, 4-1 notes)
- **Stagger fastener placement** to distribute shrinkage effect
- **Generous floor-to-floor heights** (avoid tight coordination with finishes)

**AT1.4 Application:** In module design, ensure adequate space for ceiling/MEP above floor, accounting for 10–15mm settlement over building lifetime.

### **Fire Rating Reality Check**

From WoodWorks:
- **Table 1-1 (spline):** Level I (rated)
- **Table 4-1 (bearing):** Level II (likely rated with grout/sealant)
- **Table 5-3 (ledger):** Level II–III depending on ledger size/protection

**AT1.4 Strategy:** Confirm with fire engineer early. If CLT thickness + spline detail + grout sealant achieves 60-minute rating without extra material, cost savings are substantial.

### **Acoustic Separation**

Residential connections must not create acoustic bridges. Recommendations:
- **Steel splines (Table 1-3)** provide acoustic break between panels
- **Concealed plates (Table 4-6)** isolate sound paths
- **Avoid direct wood-to-wood face contact** where sound transmission is concern

**AT1.4 Application:** Consider Table 1-3 (steel spline) for corridor-adjacent floor panels to isolate impact noise.
