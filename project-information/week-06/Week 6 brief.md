Hi all,

 

As you continue to develop your assignments I am sure you are all thinking about the First Nations Materials component of the project as noted below:

 

Your research will include first nations materials, suppliers and construction methods. You will need to consider and calculate the percentage of the structural and external cladding materials being proposed that are sourced from country or a wider context. The buildings’ structure could also reference country, i.e. could the idea of trees inform a structural grid? As part of your research you will need to create a visual map of first nations material origins/sources. 

 

Some useful resources on this topic are provided below and attached:

1. First Knowledges: Building on Country, available to purchase or as an e-book from the RMIT library

2. Gathering Space: Ngargee Djeembana (see attached)

 

And here are the some precedents that may be helpful:
ARM Munarra Centre for Regional Excellence: Use of First Nations construction company,  planning of spaces non hierarchical and well connected to outside.
Lyons Koorie heritage trust: Use of timbers sourced from Country, columns co-designed with First Nations peoples.
The Kimberwalli Centre for Excellence, NSW, BVN: use of locally sourced materials such as rammed earth and timber, orientation of building in relation to climactic conditions.
UTS National First Nations College, Warren and Mahoney, not yet complete but there might be some clues here in terms of structure and materials: https://wam.studio/work/UTS
Bunurong Land Council + Mac Rob https://jcba.com.au/projects/mac-robertson-girls-high-school 
 

For First Nations building materials suppliers I would suggest jumping online to see what you can find. A quick web search will bring up several Victorian based First Nations suppliers for hardware, building materials, etc that you may consider for your project.

 

Beyond the First Nations Materials avenue you may also consider broader Cultural Consultation through engagement via the Cultural Consultations - Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation. Take a look at the Whole of Country Plan 2025-2035 that states:

"Traditional Owners hold unceded cultural rights and responsibilities, and must be engaged in planning, decision-making, and management of Country. The work of the Wurundjeri Corporation centres on asserting and maintaining Wurundjeri Woiwurrung cultural rights and responsibilities of protecting and caring for Country. "
